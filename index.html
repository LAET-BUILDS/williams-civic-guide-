Know your rights

Everyday questions in Williams. Tap one: the short answer and its legal source come first, then the steps, deadlines and exceptions, and the lessons.

Can the city shut off my water if I'm behind on the bill?

Yes, but only after the bill has gone unpaid for at least 60 days and the city has contacted you at least 7 business days before the shut-off (Health and Safety Code §116908(a)) Verified9. It can't shut off your water while you are appealing the bill Verified9, and in some medical and money hardship cases it can't shut it off at all if you agree to a payment plan (§116910) Verified10.

What to do

  1. Call Utilities before the shut-off date: (530) 235-3274, 464 8th Street, Monday to Friday 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. Verified7.
  2. Ask for a payment arrangement. The city's written policy must offer a deferred or reduced payment plan and alternative payment schedules (Health and Safety Code §116906(a)) Verified8. Before a shut-off, the city must offer to discuss amortization, deferred or minimum payments, and a bill review (§116908(a)(1)(B)) Verified9.
  3. If you think the bill is wrong, appeal it and ask Utilities how. The policy must include a formal way to contest or appeal a bill (§116906(a)) Verified8. While an adult at the home has an appeal or bill review pending, the city can't shut off service (§116908(b)) Verified9.
  4. If a shut-off would put someone's health at risk, the shut-off can be stopped when all three of these are true: a primary care provider certifies that losing water would be life-threatening or a serious threat to the health and safety of someone living there; you show you can't pay in the normal billing cycle (for example, someone in the home gets CalWORKs, CalFresh, general assistance, Medi-Cal, SSI/SSP or WIC, or the household's income is below 200% of the federal poverty level); and you agree to a payment plan (§116910(a)) Verified10.
  5. If your water was already shut off and your household income is below 200% of the federal poverty level, the reconnection fee during normal hours is capped at $50, and $150 after hours, adjusted for inflation since Jan 1, 2021 and never more than the actual cost. The city must also waive late-payment interest once every 12 months (§116914) Verified11.
Does the state law cover Williams?
  • The state shut-off law covers "urban and community water systems," meaning public water systems with more than 200 service connections (Health and Safety Code §116902(e)) Verified12. A system like the city's, not regulated by the Public Utilities Commission, had to comply starting April 1, 2020; smaller community systems were added Aug 1, 2024 (§116904(c), (d)) Verified13.
  • The State Water Board lists the City of Williams water system (CA0610004) with 1,426 residential and 139 commercial metered connections, serving about 5,538 people Verified14. That is well over 200 connections Calculated14,12.
  • The city's own code says a residential shut-off for nonpayment must follow Health and Safety Code §116900 and the following sections, the Public Utilities Code sections on water service, and the shut-off policy the council adopts (Williams Municipal Code §13.04.261, amended Dec 16, 2020) Verified15.
Notices and deadlines
  • Bills: water charges are due 20 days after the bill date, and a 10% penalty is added the day after the due date (Williams Municipal Code §§13.04.230, 13.04.235) Verified15. Water and garbage charges on the same bill can't be separated, and being behind on any part of the bill can be cause for a shut-off (§13.04.250) Verified15.
  • 60 days: the city code allows a residential shut-off for an unpaid water, sewer or trash account within 60 days of the due date (§13.04.260(A)) Verified15. State law says no shut-off until a payment has been delinquent for at least 60 days (Health and Safety Code §116908(a)(1)) Verified9.
  • 7 business days: the city must contact the customer named on the account by phone or written notice at least 7 business days before a shut-off (§116908(a)(1)(A)) Verified9.
  • What a written notice must say: the customer's name and address; the amount owed; the date by which you must pay or make payment arrangements; how to ask for more time; how to ask for a bill review and appeal; and how to ask for a deferred, reduced or alternative payment schedule (§116908(a)(1)(C)) Verified9. If the mailing address isn't the service address, a notice must also be sent to the service address addressed to "Occupant" Verified9.
  • If the city can't reach you by phone and its written notice comes back undeliverable, it must make a good-faith effort to visit the home and leave a notice of the coming shut-off and its shut-off policy in a conspicuous place (§116908(a)(2)) Verified9. When it calls you, it must offer to send you the policy in writing (§116908(a)(1)(B)) Verified9.
  • No weekend shut-offs: the city code bars shutting off water for nonpayment on a Saturday, Sunday or legal holiday, or when city business offices are closed (Williams Municipal Code §13.04.264) Verified15.
Payment plans and hardship
  • When the three hardship conditions are met, the city must offer one or more of these: paying off the balance over time (amortization), an alternative payment schedule, a partial or full reduction of the unpaid balance paid for without extra charges to other ratepayers, or a temporary deferral of payment (§116910(b)(1)) Verified10. The city may choose which option you get and set its terms (§116910(b)(2)) Verified10. The certification can come from the customer or from a tenant of the customer (§116910(a)(1)) Verified10.
  • A payment plan normally has to pay off the unpaid balance within 12 months, though the city may allow longer to avoid undue hardship (§116910(b)(2)) Verified10.
  • If you then stay 60 days or more behind on the plan, or on your current charges while on the plan, the city may shut off service no sooner than 5 business days after posting a final notice at the home (§116910(b)(3)) Verified10.
The city's written policy
  • State law requires the policy to be in writing, posted on the city's website, and available in English and in the other languages the law lists. It must include a payment plan, alternative schedules, a formal way to appeal a bill, and a phone number to call for help (Health and Safety Code §116906(a), (b)) Verified8. The State Water Board enforces these rules (§116906(c)) Verified8.
  • The policy posted on the city's website is a one-page notice dated March 2020. It says service may be shut off after an account is 60 days or more past due and the customer has received a 15-day notice. It cites the Public Utilities Code and doesn't describe the payment-plan or bill-appeal steps Verified16. Ask Utilities to explain those options.
  • The posted policy lists different phone numbers in each language: (530) 473-2955 Ext. 111 in English and (530) 235-3270 Ext. 111 in Spanish Verified16,17. The Utilities contact page lists (530) 235-3274 Verified7.
  • Under the city code, complaints, payment-arrangement requests and appeals about residential shut-offs are handled under that policy (Williams Municipal Code §13.04.265) Verified15.
Reconnection, renters and liens
  • The city code says service is turned back on after the bill is paid in full plus a reconnection fee, which the code lists as $20 the first time, $35 the second and $50 after that (Williams Municipal Code §13.04.268(A)) Verified15. For lower-income households, the state caps above also apply (Health and Safety Code §116914) Verified11.
  • The property owner is responsible for water charges, even when a tenant uses the water. If a tenant is paying for the service, the owner's responsibility starts on a change of tenant or other transfer (Williams Municipal Code §13.04.221) Verified15. Unpaid water charges can become a lien on the property (§13.04.221) Verified15.
  • If you rent, your landlord can't shut off your water or other utilities to force you to move out (Civil Code §789.3) Verified18. See "Can my landlord raise my rent or evict me?" below.

I got a code violation notice or citation. What now?

Check the deadline first. You have 21 days from the date of an administrative citation to ask for a hearing (Williams Municipal Code §8.16.510) Verified19, and 15 days from a notice to abate to ask for one (§8.16.110) Verified20. For a building, zoning or similar problem that isn't an immediate danger, fixing it by the correction date on the citation means no fine (§8.16.500(A)) Verified19.

What to do

  1. See which paper you have. An administrative citation is a fine with a correction date (Art. VI) Verified19. A notice to abate orders you to fix a nuisance condition by a compliance date (Art. II, §8.16.110) Verified20.
  2. Fix the problem by the date if you can, and get any permits first. Under a notice to abate, if the city finds the problem fixed before the hearing, the hearing is called off (§8.16.150(A)) Verified20. Paying a citation fine doesn't replace fixing the problem (§8.16.500(A)) Verified19.
  3. To contest a citation, get a request-for-hearing form from the City Clerk and return it within 21 days of the citation date with the fine as a deposit or an approved deposit waiver (§8.16.510) Verified19. City Clerk: 810 E Street, (530) 235-3274 Verified21.
  4. If you can't afford the deposit, file a deposit-waiver request with the city finance officer within 10 days of the citation date, with a sworn statement showing you can't pay it. The answer comes within 2 business days and is final (§8.16.520) Verified19.
  5. To contest a notice to abate, file a written hearing request with the City Clerk within 15 days of the notice (§8.16.110(A)) Verified20.
  6. Go to the hearing. Not showing up forfeits a citation fine and ends your right to go to court (§8.16.570(A)) Verified19. Not showing up to an abatement hearing counts as admitting the problem exists (§8.16.120(B)) Verified20.
  7. If you lose a citation hearing, you can appeal to the Colusa County Superior Court within 20 days after the decision is served on you (Gov. Code §53069.4(b)(1); Williams Municipal Code §8.16.570(B)) Verified22,19.
  8. Questions about the violation: Code Enforcement, 700 North Street, (530) 473-2533 Verified21.
What a citation must say, and how it reaches you
  • Each citation must list the date and place of the violation; the code section and a description; the fine and, for a non-emergency health or safety violation, a correction date; how, where and by when to pay; how and by when to contest it and where to get the hearing form; the officer's name and signature; and how to ask for a deposit waiver (Williams Municipal Code §8.16.480(B)) Verified19.
  • For a property violation, the officer first tries to hand it to you. If that fails, it is sent by certified mail and counts as served 3 days after mailing. If that fails too, it is posted on the property (§8.16.485) Verified19. Refusing to sign doesn't cancel it Verified19.
  • A notice to abate is posted on the property and delivered in person or by certified mail (§8.16.110(B)) Verified20. Not receiving it doesn't make it invalid (§8.16.110(C)) Verified20.
Fines, late penalties and liens
  • Fine amounts come from a schedule the City Council sets by resolution, and it can set higher fines for repeating the same violation within 12 months (§8.16.490) Verified19.
  • Pay within 21 calendar days of the correction date (§8.16.500(A)) Verified19. A late fine adds a 10% penalty, then 10% a year interest until paid (§8.16.550) Verified19.
  • Unpaid fines are a civil debt, and if the violation is on property you own, the city can file a lien on it (§8.16.560) Verified19.
  • If the city fixes a nuisance itself after an abatement decision, the cost becomes the owner's personal obligation and can be collected as a lien or special assessment (§8.16.140) Verified20. The owner can also owe the city's administrative costs, including investigation, staff time and re-inspections (§8.16.160) Verified20.
  • Citations are in addition to other remedies, including criminal ones (§8.16.470) Verified19. Breaking a city ordinance is a misdemeanor unless the ordinance makes it an infraction (Gov. Code §36900) Verified23.
How the hearing works
  • Citation hearing: the clerk sets it 15 to 60 days after your request and tells you the time and place at least 10 days ahead (§§8.16.530(B), 8.16.510(C)) Verified19. If the officer sends the hearing officer an extra written report, you get a copy at least 5 days before (§8.16.510(D)) Verified19.
  • The city must prove, by a preponderance of the evidence, that the violation happened and that you are responsible. You can question the officer and other witnesses, present your own evidence, and bring a lawyer. Witnesses testify under oath, and formal rules of evidence don't apply (§8.16.530(D)-(F)) Verified19. The citation itself counts as evidence of the facts on it (§8.16.530(G)) Verified19.
  • The hearing officer can't be someone who worked on investigating or enforcing the case (§8.16.475(C)) Verified19. You get a written decision with reasons. If the citation is canceled or reduced, the city refunds your deposit with interest (§8.16.540) Verified19.
  • Abatement hearing: held within 45 days of your request, with at least 10 days' written notice (§8.16.110(D)) Verified20. The city must show, by a preponderance of the evidence, that a nuisance exists and the proposed fix is appropriate (§8.16.120(C)) Verified20. The written decision is due within 10 days and is sent by certified mail (§8.16.130) Verified20. If you don't ask for a hearing in time, the nuisance is treated as confirmed (§8.16.110(E)) Verified20.
Going to court
  • The court hears the case fresh ("de novo"), as a limited civil case. The city's file on the case and the citation are received as evidence (Gov. Code §53069.4(b)(1)) Verified22. You must serve a copy of your notice of appeal on the city in person or by first-class mail Verified22. You pay the filing fee set in Gov. Code §70615; if the court rules for you, the city reimburses it and refunds any deposit as the judgment says (§53069.4(b)(2)) Verified22. If no appeal is filed in time, the hearing officer's decision is final (§53069.4(c)) Verified22.
  • State law says a city must give a reasonable time to fix a continuing building, plumbing, electrical or similar structural or zoning violation that isn't an immediate danger before fining for it (§53069.4(a)(2)(A)) Verified22. When the violation would otherwise be an infraction, the administrative fine can't be more than the maximum infraction fine (§53069.4(a)(1)) Verified22.
  • Colusa County Superior Court: colusa.courts.ca.gov Verified24. We haven't confirmed which court location hears these appeals Unverified24.
If an inspector wants to come in
  • A city inspector can't enter your home without your consent or an inspection warrant signed by a judge (Camara v. Municipal Court; Code Civ. Proc. §1822.50) Verified25,26.
  • The same rule covers the parts of a business that aren't open to the public (See v. City of Seattle) Verified27.
  • An inspection warrant doesn't always need evidence of a specific violation: reasonable area-wide inspection standards can be enough (Camara) Verified25.
  • The abatement rules say the same: if the owner doesn't give written consent, the city enters with verbal permission, an inspection warrant or another lawful way (Williams Municipal Code §8.16.120(F)) Verified20.

Do I need a permit for a fence, a shed, or a yard sale?

Usually not a building permit for a fence up to 7 feet high or a one-story detached shed of 120 square feet or less: Williams uses the 2025 California Residential Code, which exempts both (Williams Municipal Code §15.06.010; California Residential Code §R105.2) Verified30,31. Being exempt from a permit doesn't exempt the work from zoning and other city rules, so check with Planning before you build Verified31. A yard sale needs no permit, but the city limits how often, when and where you can hold one (Williams Municipal Code ch. 5.15) Verified29.

What to do

  1. Before you build, call to confirm: Building, 460 8th Street, (530) 235-3276, or Planning & Zoning, 460 8th Street, (530) 235-3279 Verified21.
  2. If you need a permit, apply on the city's online Building Permit Application (iWorQ) Verified28. Work in the street or sidewalk needs an encroachment permit under Williams Municipal Code ch. 12.14, also on iWorQ Verified28.
  3. Call 811 (Underground Service Alert) at least two working days before you dig, as the city's encroachment permit requires Verified28.
  4. For a yard sale, follow the limits: no more than 3 days in a row or two weekends in a row per sale; no more than 4 sales a year at the same address or by the same person; no selling between 6 p.m. and 8 a.m. (§5.15.050) Verified29.
  5. Sell only your own things, not items bought to resell (§5.15.040) Verified29. Keep everything on your property, off the street and sidewalk, and leave room for fire and police access (§5.15.060) Verified29.
  6. Signs: no more than two, each 4 square feet or less, only on the property where the sale is. No signs on the street, utility poles, street sign poles or other public property (§5.15.070) Verified29.
What the residential code exempts
  • Williams adopted the 2025 California Residential Code, including its Chapter 1 on permits, by Ordinance 274-25 on Oct 15, 2025 (Williams Municipal Code §15.06.010, third-party copy) Verified30.
  • No building permit is needed for: one-story detached accessory structures with a floor area of 120 square feet or less (other than storm shelters); fences not over 7 feet high; retaining walls not over 4 feet, measured from the bottom of the footing, unless they hold back extra load; sidewalks and driveways; painting, flooring, cabinets and similar finish work; prefabricated pools under 24 inches deep; swings and playground equipment; and decks of 200 square feet or less that are no more than 30 inches above grade, not attached to the house and not serving a required exit door (California Residential Code §R105.2) Verified31.
  • Anything bigger than those limits, such as a fence over 7 feet or a shed over 120 square feet, isn't on the exempt list, so the general rule applies: building, enlarging or altering a structure needs a permit first (§R105.1) Verified31.
  • A permit-exempt shed may still be covered by the California Wildland-Urban Interface Code (§R105.2) Verified31.
Zoning, fees and penalties
  • We didn't find a fence-height or shed-placement rule for homes in the third-party copy of the Williams zoning code, so we can't tell you the local limits. Ask Planning & Zoning Unverified32.
  • Breaking the residential code is a misdemeanor under the city code (Williams Municipal Code §15.06.020) Verified30. Breaking the yard-sale rules is an infraction (§5.15.080) Verified29.
  • The city's fee for work done without a permit is $324.11 under Resolution 26-40, which our notes of the Oct 21, 2026 meeting video show passing 5-0. The minutes weren't approved when we checked Verified33,6.
Yard sale details
  • The rules cover any sale of personal property at a home or on residentially zoned land, including from the garage, driveway, yard, patio or house (§5.15.010) Verified29.
  • They don't apply to sales by religious or charitable groups whose proceeds go only to those purposes, to sales under a court order or other legal proceeding, or to legal nonconforming businesses in a residential zone (§5.15.030) Verified29.
  • Signs must also follow the city's sign rules in chapter 17.11 (§5.15.070) Verified29.

What are my rights if the police stop me?

The officer must tell you why you were stopped before asking you questions about a crime or traffic violation (Vehicle Code §2806.5) Verified42. You don't have to answer questions or agree to a search (U.S. Const., 5th Amend.; Miranda v. Arizona; Schneckloth v. Bustamonte) Verified38,43,39, but if you are driving you must show your license and registration when asked (Vehicle Code §§12951(b), 4462(a)) Verified35,36. Don't resist or run, even if you think the stop is wrong: willfully resisting, delaying or obstructing an officer is a crime (Penal Code §148(a)(1)) Verified34.

What to do

  1. Stay calm, keep your hands where the officer can see them, and don't physically resist (Penal Code §148(a)(1)) Verified34.
  2. If you're driving, hand over your license and registration when asked (Vehicle Code §§12951(b), 4462(a)) Verified35,36. If you get a ticket or are in a crash, you must also show proof of insurance, and you can show it on your phone. The officer may look only at the insurance proof, nothing else on the phone (§16028(b), (c), (g)) Verified37.
  3. If you don't want to answer questions, say so clearly, for example: "I'm going to stay silent." The Fifth Amendment says no one can be forced to be a witness against themselves in a criminal case (U.S. Const., 5th Amend.) Verified38.
  4. If asked to allow a search, you can say "I don't consent to a search." A search based on consent is valid only if the consent was voluntary (Schneckloth v. Bustamonte) Verified39. Don't physically block the officer.
  5. You can record the officer from a public place or anywhere you have a right to be. Recording isn't by itself a crime or a reason to detain or arrest you (Penal Code §148(g)) Verified34.
  6. If you want to file a complaint afterward, every police department must have a public complaint procedure with a written description available to the public (Penal Code §832.5(a)(1)) Verified40. Williams Police Department: (530) 473-2661 Verified21.
When police can stop and hold you
  • A brief stop needs reasonable suspicion based on "specific and articulable facts," not just a hunch (Terry v. Ohio) Verified41. The Fourth Amendment and the California Constitution protect against unreasonable searches and seizures (U.S. Const., 4th Amend.; Cal. Const., Art. I, §13) Verified38.
  • The officer must also write the reason for the stop on any ticket or police report (Vehicle Code §2806.5(a)) Verified42. The officer doesn't have to state the reason if they reasonably believe holding it back is needed to protect life or property from an imminent threat (§2806.5(b)) Verified42. This rule took effect Jan 1, 2024 Verified42.
  • An officer can't stop a car just to check whether the driver has insurance (Vehicle Code §16028(a)) Verified37.
Silence, warnings and arrest
  • Miranda warnings are required before custodial interrogation, meaning questioning after you've been taken into custody or otherwise deprived of your freedom in a significant way (Miranda v. Arizona) Verified43. They aren't required for every conversation during a stop Verified43.
  • After an arrest without a warrant, a judge generally must decide within 48 hours whether there was probable cause (County of Riverside v. McLaughlin) Verified44.
  • In California criminal cases, evidence is thrown out because of an illegal search only when the U.S. Constitution requires it (Cal. Const., Art. I, §28(f)(2); In re Lance W.) Verified38,45.
  • Resisting, delaying or obstructing an officer can bring a fine of up to $1,000, up to a year in county jail, or both (Penal Code §148(a)(1)) Verified34.
Complaints
  • Every California police agency must set up a procedure to investigate complaints from the public and make a written description of it available to the public (Penal Code §832.5(a)(1)) Verified40.
  • Complaints and the reports on them must be kept at least 5 years, or at least 15 years if the complaint is sustained (Penal Code §832.5(b)) Verified40.
  • The city's police web pages we checked on Oct 29, 2026 (the police home page, contact page, police records page and the city's Forms & Permits list) don't post the written complaint procedure or a complaint form Verified46. Ask the department for the written description at (530) 473-2661 Verified21.

Can my landlord raise my rent or evict me?

It depends on your rental. For most covered rentals, state law limits increases to 5% plus inflation, and never more than 10%, over any 12 months (Civil Code §1947.12) Verified48, and after you've lived there 12 months the landlord needs a reason the law allows ("just cause") to end your tenancy (Civil Code §1946.2) Verified49. Common exemptions include housing built in the last 15 years and single-family homes or condos not owned by a corporation or REIT, if you were given a written exemption notice (Civil Code §§1947.12(d), 1946.2(e)) Verified48,49. Either way, a landlord can't lock you out, remove doors or cut off utilities to force you out; an eviction goes through court (Civil Code §789.3) Verified18,51.

What to do

  1. Check the notice of a rent increase. On a month-to-month rental it must be in writing, at least 30 days ahead for an increase of 10% or less over 12 months, or at least 90 days ahead for more than 10% (Civil Code §827(b)) Verified47.
  2. Check whether your rental is exempt. If the landlord claims the single-family home or condo exemption, you must have been given the exact written exemption statement the law requires (Civil Code §1947.12(d)(5)) Verified48. Look in your lease.
  3. Do the math. The cap is measured from the lowest rent charged during the past 12 months, and there can be no more than two increases in 12 months (§1947.12(a)) Verified48. Colusa County isn't one of the metro areas named in the law, so the inflation figure is the California Consumer Price Index published by the Department of Industrial Relations (§1947.12(g)(1)(B)) Verified48.
  4. If you get a notice to end your tenancy, check that it states a reason the law allows (§1946.2(a)) Verified49. If the problem can be fixed, the landlord must first give you a notice and a chance to fix it (§1946.2(c)) Verified49. For a no-fault reason, the landlord owes you one month's rent in relocation help or must waive your last month's rent (§1946.2(d)) Verified49.
  5. If you are served court papers for an eviction (an "unlawful detainer"), you have 10 days to file a response, not counting Saturdays, Sundays and court holidays (Code Civ. Proc. §1167) Verified50. If you don't respond, the judge can decide the case without hearing from you Verified51. The California Courts Self-Help Guide has the forms Verified51, and the Colusa County Superior Court handles local cases Verified24.
  6. If you are locked out or your utilities are cut off to force you out, the landlord can owe your actual damages plus up to $100 for each day, at least $250, and attorney fees (Civil Code §789.3) Verified18.
The rent cap (Civil Code §1947.12)
  • Over any 12 months, rent can't go up more than 5% plus the change in the cost of living, or 10%, whichever is lower, measured from the lowest rent charged in the previous 12 months (§1947.12(a)(1)) Verified48. No more than two increases are allowed in that period (§1947.12(a)(2)) Verified48.
  • When a new tenancy starts, the landlord can set any starting rent; the cap applies to later increases (§1947.12(b)) Verified48.
  • Not covered: deed-restricted affordable housing; dorms; housing under a stricter local rent-control law; housing with a certificate of occupancy issued in the last 15 years (unless it's a mobilehome); a single-family home or condo whose owner isn't a real estate investment trust, a corporation or an LLC with a corporate member, if the tenant got the written exemption notice; and a duplex where the owner lives in the other unit (§1947.12(d)) Verified48.
  • A landlord who charges more than the cap can owe the overcharge, up to three times that amount if willful, plus attorney fees. The claim must be filed within 3 years (§1947.12(k)) Verified48. Any lease clause waiving these rights is void (§1947.12(l)) Verified48.
  • This version of the law took effect April 1, 2024 and is set to end Jan 1, 2030 Verified48.
Just cause to end a tenancy (Civil Code §1946.2)
  • Just cause is required once every tenant has lived there continuously for 12 months, or, if a new adult tenant joined, once at least one tenant has lived there 24 months or every tenant 12 months (§1946.2(a)) Verified49. The reason must be stated in the written notice Verified49.
  • At-fault reasons include not paying rent; breaking a material term of the lease after a written notice to correct it; creating a nuisance; damaging the property; refusing to sign a similar new lease; criminal activity on the property; subletting against the lease; and refusing the landlord lawful entry (§1946.2(b)(1)) Verified49.
  • No-fault reasons include the owner or a close family member moving in (they must move in within 90 days and live there at least 12 months); taking the unit off the rental market; a government or court order; and demolishing or substantially remodeling the unit, which must take at least 30 days and requires the landlord to give you a copy of the permits (§1946.2(b)(2)) Verified49.
  • For a no-fault reason, the relocation payment equals one month's rent and must be paid within 15 days of the notice, or the landlord can instead waive the last month's rent in writing. A notice that doesn't comply is void (§1946.2(d)) Verified49.
  • Not covered (besides the same kinds of housing exempt from the rent cap): a room where you share a bathroom or kitchen with the owner who lives there; an owner-occupied single-family home renting out no more than two units or bedrooms; and housing with a certificate of occupancy issued in the last 15 years (§1946.2(e)) Verified49.
  • A landlord who breaks this law can owe actual damages, and up to three times that if willful (§1946.2(h)) Verified49.
Notice periods and the court process
  • To end a month-to-month tenancy, the landlord must give at least 60 days' written notice if every tenant has lived there a year or more, or 30 days if anyone has lived there less than a year (Civil Code §1946.1) Verified52. The landlord can't charge you for serving the notice (§1946.1) Verified52.
  • The court process is: a written notice, then a court case if you don't do what the notice says, then your response, then the judge's decision. It can take 30 to 45 days or longer from the notice Verified51.
  • If you lose, you can move out or ask the court for more time. If you don't move, the landlord can ask the sheriff to remove you Verified51.
  • The landlord can't remove your belongings, doors or windows, change the locks, or cut off water, heat, light or other utilities to make you leave (Civil Code §789.3) Verified18.

Can the city raise my water rate?

Yes, but only after mailing notice to property owners and holding a public hearing at least 45 days later, and not if a majority of the affected owners file written protests (California Constitution, Art. XIII D, §6(a)) Verified38. The rate also can't bring in more than the service costs (§6(b)) Verified38.

What to do

  1. Watch for the mailed notice and the hearing date. The notice goes to the record owner of each affected parcel and must give the amount, how it was calculated, the reason, and the hearing date, time and place (Art. XIII D, §6(a)(1)) Verified38. Council agendas are posted at least 72 hours before regular meetings (Gov. Code §54954.2) Verified53, and Agenda Watch flags fee items.
  2. If you own the property, you can protest in writing. At the hearing the city must consider all protests, and it can't impose the charge if written protests come from a majority of the owners of the affected parcels (§6(a)(2)) Verified38. Ask the City Clerk, (530) 235-3274, how and where to turn in a protest Verified21.
  3. If you rent, the mailed notice goes to the owner, not to you (§6(a)(1)) Verified38. You can still speak on the item during the hearing (Gov. Code §54954.3) Verified54.
The limits on what the city charges
  • Money from the charge can't exceed the cost of providing the service, can't be used for anything else, and can't be more than the proportional cost of serving your parcel (Art. XIII D, §6(b)(1)-(3)) Verified38. You can't be charged for a service you don't use or that isn't immediately available to you (§6(b)(4)) Verified38. If someone sues over a charge, the city has to prove it followed these rules (§6(b)) Verified38.
  • Water, sewer and trash charges don't need the separate vote of property owners or residents that other property-related fees need, but they still need the notice and protest hearing (§6(c)) Verified38.
