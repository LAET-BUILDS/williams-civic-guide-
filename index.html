Know your rights

Common questions in Williams. Tap one for the short answer and the full lesson.

Can the city raise my water rate?
  • Yes, but not quietly. A new or higher water, sewer or trash charge needs mailed notice to parcel owners and a public hearing at least 45 days later. If a majority of the affected owners protest in writing, the city can't impose it (Art. XIII D, §6(a)) Verified7.
  • Water, sewer and trash skip the separate election other property-related fees need, but not the protest hearing (§6(c)) Verified7.
  • The money raised can't exceed the cost of the service or be diverted to other uses (§6(b)) Verified7.
  • Lesson: Art. XIII D (Prop. 218)
  • Williams fees vs. Prop. 218
  • Williams water and sewer rates
Can I record a council meeting?
  • Yes. Anyone attending an open meeting has the right to record it on audio or video (Gov. Code §54953.5(a)) Verified8.
  • The council can stop a recording only if it reasonably finds that its noise, light or blocked view would persistently disrupt the meeting Verified8.
  • The city's own recordings are public records, but it may erase them after 30 days (§54953.5(b)) Verified8. Williams posts meeting videos online Verified9.
  • Lesson: the Brown Act
  • Art. IV §7: the same right at the Legislature
Can I speak about anything at a meeting?
  • About anything within the council's jurisdiction. The council must give the public a chance to speak on those matters (Gov. Code §54954.3) Verified10.
  • It may set reasonable time limits, but it may not prohibit criticism of the city's policies or actions. Speakers who use a translator get twice the time Verified10.
  • If your topic isn't on the agenda, the council can listen, but generally can't act on it at that meeting Verified11.
  • Lesson: First Amendment and public comment
  • How to speak in Williams
Can a city inspector come into my home?
  • Not without your consent or an inspection warrant signed by a judge (Camara v. Municipal Court; Code Civ. Proc. §1822.50) Verified12,13.
  • The same rule covers the parts of a business that aren't open to the public Verified14.
  • An inspection warrant doesn't always need evidence of a specific violation: reasonable area-wide inspection standards can be enough Verified12.
  • Lesson: Fourth Amendment and inspections
  • Williams: entry and inspection
How do I get city records?
  • Ask the city clerk, 810 E Street, (530) 235-3274 Verified4. You don't have to say why you want them (Gov. Code §7921.300) Verified15.
  • The city must tell you within 10 days whether it has records it can release, and can extend that by up to 14 days in unusual circumstances. The 10 days is to decide, not necessarily to hand everything over Verified16.
  • The right to see public officials' writings is in the California Constitution (Art. I, §3(b)(1)) Verified7.
  • Lesson: the Public Records Act
  • Public Records Act guide
Can the council meet in private?
  • Generally no. "All meetings of the legislative body of a local agency shall be open and public" (Gov. Code §54953(a)) Verified17, and open meetings are a right in the California Constitution (Art. I, §3(b)(1)) Verified7.
  • Closed sessions are allowed only for listed topics, for example some personnel matters, labor negotiations, litigation and real estate Unverified18. Certain actions taken in closed session must be reported publicly Verified19.
  • Regular meeting agendas must be posted at least 72 hours ahead Verified11.
  • Lesson: the Brown Act

How Williams is governed

Williams is a general law city: the city says it “was made a General Law City on May 20, 1920” Verified20. That means state law sets many of the rules the city must follow.
