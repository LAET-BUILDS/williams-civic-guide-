The Ralph M. Brown Act (open meetings law)

On this page (3 sections)
  1. Unofficial plain-language summary
  2. Using it in Williams
  3. Sources cited on this page

Official text: California Government Code, Title 5, Division 2, Part 1, Chapter 9, starting at §54950 on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov Verified1. We opened the official text there on October 29, 2026. The full text is not copied here; open the link to read it.

The California Constitution also guarantees access: "the meetings of public bodies and the writings of public officials and agencies shall be open to public scrutiny" (Art. I, §3(b)(1)) Verified2. Read Article I, Section 3.

Unofficial plain-language summary

Unofficial summary Not legal advice; the statute controls.

Topic Section What it says (summary) Label
Purpose §54950 Local agencies exist to do the people's business, and their actions and deliberations should be done openly. Verified3
Agenda posting §54954.2 Regular meeting agendas must be posted at least 72 hours before the meeting, with a brief description of each item. Generally, no action on items not on the agenda. Verified4,5
Public comment §54954.3 The public must have a chance to address the body on items within its jurisdiction. Reasonable time limits are allowed; criticism may not be prohibited; speakers using a translator get double time. Verified6
Special meetings §54956 Generally require 24 hours' notice. Verified7
Closed sessions §54962, §54956.8, §54956.9, §54957, §54957.6 No closed session unless the law expressly allows it (§54962). Allowed topics include real estate negotiations (§54956.8), pending litigation (§54956.9), security threats and some personnel matters (§54957), and labor negotiations (§54957.6). Verified8,9,10,11,12
Reporting closed-session actions §54957.1 Certain actions taken in closed session must be reported publicly. Verified13
Enforcement §54960 Anyone can sue to stop or prevent violations. Verified14
Cure or correct §54960.1 Before suing to void an action, send a written demand: within 90 days (30 days for certain agenda violations); the body has 30 days to respond; then 15 days to sue. Verified15

"Verified" above means we read that section's text ourselves: rows citing a leginfo source were read on the official site (October 29, 2026); the others on an unofficial copy (California.Public.Law). The statute controls; check the official text before relying on it.
