Meetings

The city says regular council meetings are on the third Wednesday of each month at 6:00 PM in the Council Chambers at City Hall, 810 E Street. Special meetings and study sessions are scheduled as needed Verified1.

Read the agenda and packet

  • Official agenda page: cityofwilliams.org agendas and minutes Verified2. The agenda packet PDF has the agenda plus staff reports.
  • The October 21, 2026 packet has an English and a Spanish agenda Verified3.
  • The Brown Act requires a regular meeting's agenda to be posted at least 72 hours ahead Verified4.
  • Agenda Watch on this site goes through every agenda item in plain language and flags money, contracts, pay and fees.
  • Many older packets are scanned images, so their text can't be searched Verified2.
  • New ordinances show up on agendas before they reach the code. For example, the October 21, 2026 agenda includes the first reading of Ordinance 282-26 Verified3.

  • Livestream and past videos: AV Capture All video portal Verified5. The agenda calls the livestream "a courtesy" Verified3, so attend in person if it matters to you.
  • We did not find a way to comment remotely in the October 21, 2026 agenda Verified3. Ask the city clerk whether written comments are accepted: (530) 235-3274 Verified1.

Public comment

  • General public comment comes near the start of the agenda (item D on the October 21, 2026 agenda). It is for items not listed as public hearings. To speak on an item that is on the agenda, wait until that item comes up Verified3.
  • The agenda asks speakers to use the podium and give their name, whether they live in Williams, and any organization they represent. The mayor may set a time limit depending on how many people want to speak Verified3.
  • State law: the council must give the public a chance to speak on matters within its jurisdiction. It may set reasonable time limits but may not prohibit criticism of the city's policies or actions. Speakers who use a translator get twice the time (Gov. Code §54954.3) Verified6. Courts have upheld shorter time limits Unverified7.
  • The council generally cannot act on something that is not on the agenda, so a comment can raise an issue but usually can't get a vote that night Verified4.
  • Asking for an item on a future agenda is a request, not a right. Meeting procedures are set by council resolution (WMC §2.04.020, third-party copy) Verified8.

Tips: read the packet a few days ahead; write down the item number; bring a short written copy of your comment for the city clerk so it is in the record.

Brown Act: open meetings

The Brown Act is California's open-meetings law (Gov. Code §54950 and following) Verified9. The California Constitution also says "the meetings of public bodies and the writings of public officials and agencies shall be open to public scrutiny" (Art. I, §3(b)(1)) Verified10.

Cure-and-correct demand

If the council takes an action in a way that breaks certain open-meeting rules, you can ask it to undo the action (Gov. Code §54960.1) Verified11:

  1. Send a written demand to the council describing the action and the violation.
  2. Deadline: 90 days after the action, or 30 days if the problem is that the action was not on a properly posted agenda for a meeting held in open session.
  3. The council has 30 days to cure or refuse. Silence counts as refusal.
  4. You then have 15 days to file in court.

There are exceptions (for example, substantial compliance, some contracts relied on in good faith, and tax and debt actions) Verified11.

More: Brown Act guide, section by section.

Public records

  • Under the California Public Records Act, you can ask for city records. The city must tell you within 10 days whether it has disclosable records, and can extend that by up to 14 days in unusual circumstances. The 10 days is to decide, not necessarily to hand everything over Verified12.
  • If records are wrongly withheld, you can go to court, and a winning requester can recover attorney's fees Verified13.
  • Where to send a request: the city clerk, 810 E Street, Williams, (530) 235-3274 Verified1. Describe the records as clearly as you can, keep a copy, and note the date you sent it.

More: Public Records Act guide.

Initiative and referendum

Referendum: challenge a new ordinance

  • A referendum petition must be filed within 30 days of the date the adopted ordinance is attested by the city clerk, signed by 10% of the city's voters. The ordinance is then suspended and the council must reconsider it (Elec. Code §9237) Verified14. If the council does not repeal it entirely, it must put it to a vote (§9241) Verified15.
  • It works on ordinances only, not resolutions. Budgets, many fees and many contracts are adopted by resolution Unverified16.
  • Williams development agreements are expressly subject to the 30-day referendum window (WMC §17.09, third-party copy) Verified8.

Initiative: propose a new ordinance

  • An initiative petition needs signatures of 10% of registered voters. The council must adopt the measure, put it on the ballot, or order a report first (Elec. Code §9215) Verified17.
  • Williams had 2,276 registered voters in the Secretary of State's September 4, 2026 report Verified18. At that count, 10% means at least 228 signatures Calculated18,19. The count that applies is the county's last report of registration when the petition notice is published (§9215) Verified19, so confirm the number with Colusa County Elections Unverified20.
  • The California Constitution lets city voters use initiative and referendum "under procedures that the Legislature shall provide" (Art. II, §11) Verified10.
