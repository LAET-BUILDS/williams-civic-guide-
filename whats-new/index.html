Site updates

  • Oct 29: New page: How power works in Williams. It separates formal authority (the five-member council, which decides by three votes and hires and fires the City Manager and City Attorney) from practical leverage (the staff, budget drafts, staff reports and legal advice the council relies on), and lists the tools residents can use at each step, with a source for every point. It is now in the menu and near the top of the home page, next to the three layers of law diagram. Know your rights now has six detailed answers: water rates, recording a meeting, speaking at a meeting, inspectors at the door, public records and closed sessions. Each opens to the text of the law, the steps and deadlines, court cases, what applies in Williams, what to do if the rules aren't followed, and common misconceptions. The home page now has one search, the Search button in the header.
  • Oct 29: A simpler site. The menu now has six sections: Get it done, This week at City Hall, City records, How power works, Your rights (the Constitution Course and Know your rights) and Take part. Every other page is in the footer. The home page shows the next meeting and the Constitution Course first, with a Start or Continue button, then how power works and the three layers of law, then three big buttons. Source markers are smaller (✓ read in the source, ? not confirmed, Σ calculated), and lesson links on agenda items now say “Lesson”. Every old address still works.
  • Oct 29: New Get it done page: one-tap links to the city's business license and permit applications, public records requests, water service, forms and department phones, each checked on October 29, 2026. We also confirmed more facts against official sources, including the 2,276 registered voters in Williams, how the mayor is chosen, and the Brown Act and Public Records Act sections, and made two corrections.
  • Oct 28: The home page is now a city portal: the next council meeting, what the council decided at its latest meeting, quick links to city records, Who decides and Take part, and six “Know your rights” answers. Agenda Watch items and City records sections now link to “The rule behind this” in the course, and the course has its own Learn tab that shows your progress.
  • Oct 27: The Constitution Course is now grouped into 13 topic units, starting with what residents run into most: public comment, voting, the city council, police and courts, and taxes and fees. Every lesson keeps its address, and a Browse by article index keeps the documents' own order. Sixteen short lessons were also expanded to full depth with an "In Williams" section, including the Sixteenth through Twenty-seventh Amendments, the Third, Ninth and Eleventh Amendments, and Article VII.
  • Oct 26: Added this What's new page and a contact email for corrections on the Reference page.
  • Oct 23: New City records section: ordinances and resolutions, fees and rates, the budget, and planning, in plain terms.
  • Oct 23: The Constitution Course: the full U.S. and California Constitutions in 68 lessons, and how Williams fits under them.
  • Oct 23: A Spanish edition of the main pages. The translations are still waiting for review by a native speaker.
