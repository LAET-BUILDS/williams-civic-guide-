The Method

Check a city rule in a few minutes.

Open the section in the official code Verified3, then ask:

  1. 1

    Does state law already cover it?

    City rules must not be “in conflict with general laws” (Cal. Const. art. XI, §7) Verified1.

    • When a city rule loses
    • State vs. city
    • CA Art. XI
  2. 2

    Does it limit one of your rights?

    Speech, searches, property, fair process, equal treatment:

    • 1st: speech
    • 4th: searches
    • 5th: property
    • 14th: fair process, equality
    • CA speech
    • CA property
    • CA due process
  3. 3

    How is it enforced?

    Who enforces it, what is the penalty, and do you get a hearing? See the “General Penalty” chapter (ch. 1.12) Verified2.

    • Who decides?
    • Get enforcement records

Still unsure? Ask. City Clerk: (530) 235-3274 Verified4. If it affects you personally, talk to a licensed California attorney.

See it done on two real sections: a worked path. Education, not legal advice. Only a court can decide whether a rule is valid.

    Step 1. Find it

    Start from the official code, not a copy, and note how current it is.

    Step 1: Find it

    Source: Official code: Municode Verified3. The GoCodebook copy is easier to search but is not official Verified2. City Clerk: (530) 235-3274 Verified4.

  2. 2

    Step 2. Read it

    Read the whole section slowly. Write the answers in your own words.

    Step 2: Read it

    Source: Use the official text from step 1. Look up unfamiliar legal words in the glossary.

    0/5 Next: Step 3, Check state law ↓

  3. 3

    Step 3. Check state law

    A city rule sits under state law. Look for state statutes on the same subject.

    Step 3: Check state law

    Source: The California Constitution lets a city make local rules “not in conflict with general laws” (art. XI, §7) Verified1. Official statutes: leginfo Unverified5. See Three layers of law.

    0/4 Next: Step 4, Check rights ↓

  4. 4

    Step 4. Check rights

    Ask whether the rule touches a right protected by the U.S. or California Constitution. These are questions to ask, not answers.

    Step 4: Check rights

    Source: U.S. Constitution: First, Fourth and Fourteenth Amendments Verified6,7. California Constitution: art. I, §§2, 3, 7 and 13 Verified1.

    0/4 Next: Step 5, Check court cases ↓

  5. 5

    Step 5. Check court cases

    Courts decide what the words mean. Never rely on a case you have not confirmed exists.

    Step 5: Check court cases

    Source: Find and confirm cases on CourtListener, a free, unofficial case-law database Verified8.

    0/4 Next: Step 6, Check enforcement ↓

  6. 6

    Step 6. Check enforcement

    How a rule is enforced matters as much as what it says.

    Step 6: Check enforcement

    Source: The code has a chapter titled “General Penalty” (ch. 1.12) in the third-party copy Verified2; confirm on Municode. Ask for records with the Public Records Act Unverified9.

    0/5 Next: Step 7, Ask ↓

  7. 7

    Step 7. Ask

    Take your questions to the people who write and apply the rule.

    Step 7: Ask

    Source: City Clerk: (530) 235-3274 Verified4. The Council must let the public speak on matters within its jurisdiction and may set reasonable time limits (Gov. Code §54954.3) Verified10. See Take part.

    0/4 Next: write down what is still unknown ↓

Note where and when you read each thing; codes change. If a deadline might apply to you, talk to an attorney early.
