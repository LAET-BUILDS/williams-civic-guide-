The city budget in plain terms

The city's fiscal year runs July 1 to June 30, so "FY2026" means July 2025 to June 2026. The General Fund pays for everyday services like police, parks and planning.

Key takeaways

  • FY2026 General Fund: $5,744,157 in, $5,702,810 out, +$41,347 Verified1.
  • Police is about 41% of spending. Sales and property taxes bring in the most Verified1Calculated1.
  • FY2025 audit: the balance fell from $8.8 million to $6.0 million after the city paid off CalPERS pension debt Verified2.
  • Status: approved as "preliminary" Jul 16, 2025; we found no final adoption vote Verified3.

The General Fund, FY2026 and FY2027

Lessons: Art. XIII B: the spending limit · Art. XIII A: property tax (Prop. 13)

Money in, money out, and the balanceTwo-year budget, Exhibit A
Fiscal year Money in Money out Difference
FY2026 (Jul 2025–Jun 2026) $5,744,157 $5,702,810 +$41,347
FY2027 (Jul 2026–Jun 2027) $5,903,287 $5,862,932 +$40,355

Source: two-year budget, Exhibit A, PDF page 7 Verified1. Projected FY2026 revenue is 7.4% below the FY2025 budget of $6,200,653 Calculated1.

The Jul 16, 2025 minutes say the small surpluses depended on staff furloughs, on skipping a 3% cost-of-living raise planned for Jul 1, 2025, and on pausing pension catch-up (UAL) payments, which saves $224,178 a year. They put the General Fund reserve at about $4.3 million after setting aside about $1.2 million for E Street. Minus the Streets fund deficit of $1,420,000, that left $2,880,000, or 50.5% of FY2026 spending Verified3.

What actually happened: the FY2025 audit

Lessons: The Public Records Act · Cal. Const. Art. I §3

Audited results for the year ended June 30, 2025In the Sep 16, 2026 agenda packet
General Fund, FY2025 Final budget Actual
Revenue $6,244,028 $6,128,316
Spending $8,145,035 $8,410,221

The fund balance went from $8,816,153 to $6,035,850, a drop of $2,780,303. Spending rose $3,327,744 over FY2024, mostly because of the payments that paid off the city's unfunded CalPERS pension liability. The General Fund's share was $2,817,856 (PDF page 161; staff report, PDF page 149) Verified2. The auditor's report is dated Jul 29, 2026. It gave "unmodified" (clean) opinions on the financial statements (PDF page 238) Verified2.

Our reading: The city used savings to pay off pension debt early. That made FY2025 look like a big deficit year, but it is also why the FY2026 budget could pause those payments.

How the budget gets made

Lessons: Art. XIII B: the spending limit · Art. XIII A: property tax (Prop. 13)

The rules and where to find the documentsMunicipal code ch. 3.20

The municipal code requires the Council to adopt a city budget that estimates revenue and spending, and to hold public hearings on it (§§3.20.010, 3.20.040) Verified4. The official code is on Municode, which blocks automated readers, so we read a third-party copy. See the Municode note.

  • The budget, audits and impact fee reports are on the city's financial statements page Verified5.
  • The budget PDF is a scan. Exhibit A calls it a "Preliminary" budget, and the department pages say "Proposed" Verified1.
  • Budget items show up on Council agendas. Watch for them in Agenda Watch.

This is unofficial education, not legal or financial advice. We copied every figure from the document named under its table. If a number matters to you, check it in the source PDF.
