Lesson 6: Per household or per person?

On this page (4 sections)
  1. The numbers
  2. Try it with Measure F
  3. Try it
  4. Sources cited on this page

DRAFT. This lesson is not final. It does not say which number is right.

The idea: be careful with averages. Sowell's Economic Facts and Fallacies takes on common mistakes in how people read economic facts, including about income differences Unverified1. One reason numbers mislead is that "household" and "person" are not the same size. A home can have one earner or five people. So two true numbers can tell different stories.

Key takeaways

  • Williams median household income is $90,761, with a large margin of error (±$22,136) Verified2.
  • Williams income per person is $28,552, about 58% of California's Verified3Calculated3.
  • Households here average 3.46 people Verified4.

The numbers

U.S. Census Bureau, American Community Survey, 5-year estimates for 2020 to 2024 (in 2024 dollars):

Measure Williams California
Median household income $90,761 (±$22,136) Verified2 $99,122 (±$310)
Income per person (per capita) $28,552 (±$5,071) Verified3 $49,513 (±$168)
People per household 3.46 (±0.38) Verified4

Median household income. The "±" is the margin of error. The Williams range, $68,625 to $112,897, includes California's $99,122 Calculated2. So this measure does not clearly show Williams above or below the state.

Income per person. Even the high end of the Williams range, $33,623, is well below California's $49,513 Calculated3. Households here are large, so income that looks near the state's per household is shared by more people.

Same data, two stories. Someone who wants to say "Williams is doing fine" can point to the household number. Someone who wants to say "Williams is low-income" can point to the per-person number. Both numbers are real. Which one fits depends on the question you are asking. Asking "how big is the margin of error?" is a good habit with any single-town number.

A third number. The State Water Board's 2026 preliminary affordability data lists the City of Williams water system as a "disadvantaged community," using a figure it calls "MHI with Margin of Error Considered" of $73,471 Verified5. That file defines a disadvantaged community as one where median household income is under 80% of the statewide level Verified5. This is the water system's service area, not the city's Census boundary, and the state uses its own method. So the figures differ.

Try it with Measure F

From Lesson 4: $1,200,000 ÷ 5,585 residents ≈ $215 per person (C). Multiply by the average household size and you get about $743 per household (C) Calculated6,7,4. It is the same total, but $215 and $743 sound very different. Both assume residents pay all of it, which Lesson 4 says may not be true (U).

Try it

  1. Is the number per person, per household, or per taxpayer?
  2. What is the margin of error?
  3. Which number would the other side pick, and why?
