Lesson 3: One-time money or ongoing money?

On this page (3 sections)
  1. The numbers
  2. Try it
  3. Sources cited on this page

DRAFT. This lesson is not final.

The idea: "and then what?" Sowell's Applied Economics: Thinking Beyond Stage One looks at a policy's later effects, not just the first one Unverified1. "Stage one" is what happens right away. "Stage two" is what happens next. For money, the first question is: is this a one-time amount or does it repeat every year?

Key takeaways

  • A one-time payment helps this year and is gone next year. A cost or a saving that repeats keeps going.
  • In FY2025 the city paid far more into its pension plan than the plan required. That was a big one-time payment Verified2.
  • Staff warned in 2025 that using vacancy savings "is not a long-term solution" Verified3.

The numbers

A big one-time payment. The city's FY2025 audit shows that in FY2025 the city's contributions to its CalPERS pension plans were $5,271,921, against a required $695,659. That is $4,576,262 more than required. The General Fund's share of paying off the unfunded pension debt was $2,817,856 Verified2. This is the plan-wide schedule in the audit, and we did not split it by fund beyond that.

Stage one and stage two. Stage one: the pension debt was paid. Stage two, from the same audit: the General Fund's net reserves fell 33.4% in that year, and staff link that drop to the payoff Verified2. Another stage-two item is in the June 18, 2025 budget plan, which lists the General Fund's share of pension debt payments as frozen for two years, worth $224,178 a year in FY2026 and FY2027 Verified3.

A one-time item in the same plan. The June 18, 2025 plan to close the gap lists several steps. One of them is explicitly one-time: "Anticipated One Time Use of Loss Prevention Rebate Funding" of $105,000 in FY2026 Verified3. In the same report, staff said using savings from vacant positions could balance the budget, but "recognizes this is not a long-term solution" Verified3.

A one-time payment in a pay agreement. The July 2026 amendment to the Miscellaneous Employees Association agreement runs July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2028. In place of a cost-of-living raise, it pays each covered employee a one-time gross payment of $3,000 Verified4.

Our reading (not a verdict): A one-time payment costs once. A raise to base pay costs every year after. Both can be reasonable choices. The question is what happens in the years after the money is spent, for example when the agreement ends on June 30, 2028.

Try it

  1. Is this item one-time or does it repeat?
  2. If it is one-time, what pays for the same thing next year?
  3. If it repeats, where does the money come from each year?
