Lesson 1: Where city money comes from

On this page (4 sections)
  1. The numbers
  2. The trade-offs in real life
  3. Try it
  4. Sources cited on this page

DRAFT. This lesson is not final.

The idea: scarcity and trade-offs. Sowell starts from a simple point: people want more than there is, so every choice gives something up Unverified1. In A Conflict of Visions and The Vision of the Anointed, he contrasts a view that hard problems can be "solved" with a view that they are trade-offs, where you choose which cost to accept Unverified2,3. A city budget is a good place to see this. The money is limited, so each dollar spent on one thing is not spent on another.

Key takeaways

  • Two sales tax lines make up about 44% of what the city budgeted to bring in for FY2026 Calculated4.
  • The city's audit says its sales tax is "very volatile" Verified5.
  • In June 2025 the city closed a budget gap by trading away pay and other items. Those are the trade-offs Verified6.

The numbers

The General Fund is the pot that pays for everyday services like police, parks and planning. For FY2026 (July 2025 to June 2026), the city budgeted $5,744,157 coming in. Two of the lines are sales taxes Verified4:

Source, FY2026 budget Amount
Sales and use tax (state formula) $1,598,567
Local sales tax $949,662
Both together $2,548,229
All General Fund revenue $5,744,157

Together the two sales tax lines are about 44% of the total Calculated4. The same lines were budgeted at $3,024,479 for FY2025. The FY2026 budget expects $476,250 less, or 15.7% less Calculated4.

What actually came in fell too. The city's FY2025 audit says sales tax revenue was $2,428,890 in FY2023 and $1,753,323 in FY2025, a drop of $675,567, or 27.8%. Hotel tax (transient occupancy tax) went from $528,284 to $430,276, down 18.6% Verified5. The audit gives reasons: the earlier years were boosted by pandemic spending, and a large part of Williams sales tax comes from gasoline sales, so it goes up and down with demand and gas prices Verified5.

The trade-offs in real life

In June 2025, staff reported shortfalls of $1,170,146 for FY2026 and $1,302,780 for FY2027. They said two negotiated actions would help the city avoid layoffs: a 10% furlough for all employees and ending a 3% cost-of-living raise planned for July 1, 2025. With those, the shortfalls shrank to $65,159 and $229,669 Verified6. The staff's list of other steps also included leaving a Public Works Director position unfilled, leaving one police officer position vacant, deferring a Housing Element update, and using a one-time rebate Verified6.

Our reading (not a verdict): Each step on that list gave something up. That is what Sowell means by a trade-off. Whether the city chose well is a question for residents and the Council. The lesson is to look for the list of what was given up.

Try it

  1. If sales tax falls again, which of the items above could the city give up next? What would be lost?
  2. Which lines of the budget would you want the city to protect? What would you give up to protect them?
  3. Where in the packet or budget could you check?
