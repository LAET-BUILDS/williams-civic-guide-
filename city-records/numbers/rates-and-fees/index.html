Lesson 5: Rates and fees, prices as signals

On this page (4 sections)
  1. The numbers
  2. How the decision was made
  3. Try it
  4. Sources cited on this page

DRAFT. This lesson is not final. It does not say whether any rate is right or wrong.

The idea: prices as signals. Sowell's Basic Economics is built around how prices work. A price tells people what something costs to provide, and it influences how much of it they use Unverified1. A city rate is a price, set by a vote instead of by a market. So you can ask what the rate tells people, and who it asks to cover the cost.

Key takeaways

  • A typical home's fixed water and sewer charges go from $117.37 a month in Dec 2024 to $184.68 in Feb 2030 Calculated2.
  • Part of the bill is fixed. Part depends on how much water you use.
  • 328 written protests were counted. 828 were needed to stop the increase Verified3.

The numbers

The city's "New rates 2026" exhibits set the schedule. For a home with a small (5/8 to 3/4 inch) meter Verified2:

Charge Dec 2024 Feb 2026 Feb 2030
Water, fixed monthly $25.33 $34.20 $65.01
Sewer, home, fixed monthly $92.04 $99.40 $119.67
Both fixed charges $117.37 $133.60 $184.68
Water, per hundred cubic feet (hcf) above 5 $2.23 $3.01 $5.72
Sewer, per hcf above 5 $2.75 $2.97 $3.58

From Dec 2024 to Feb 2030, the two fixed charges rise $67.31 a month, or 57%. The water fixed charge and the water per-hcf rate each rise about 157%. The sewer rates each rise about 30% Calculated2. The base charges include up to 5 hcf of water a month. The per-hcf rates apply above that Verified2.

What the price tells people. The fixed charge stays the same no matter how much water you use. The per-hcf charge goes up when you use more. That is the part of the rate that "signals" the cost of the next unit of water. Whether a rate should lean on one or the other is a design choice, and each choice has trade-offs: a fixed charge gives the city steadier income, and a per-use charge gives customers a reason to save. We list this as a way to think, not as a finding about Williams.

How the decision was made

Water and sewer charges are property-related fees under Proposition 218, so they need a public hearing where owners can file written protests Verified3. At the January 21, 2026 hearing, staff reported that 328 written protests had been received, and that a majority protest would need 828 valid protests. Staff said that even if all 328 were valid, the total was well below 828. The Council then adopted Resolution 26-01, 5-0 Verified3.

Staff said the increases were needed because of insufficient revenues to fund ongoing operations, rising regulatory costs, and the need for long-term financial stability. Speakers raised affordability, especially for low-income households and farmworker families Verified3. The FY2026 budget showed the water fund taking in $1,225,441 and spending $1,318,378 Verified4.

Our reading (not a verdict): A price below the cost of a service gives one signal, and a price that covers it gives another. Either way, someone bears the cost: the customers, the taxpayers, or a later year. The lesson is to ask which one.

Try it

  1. What does this rate tell a customer about the cost of the next unit?
  2. Who pays if the rate does not cover the cost?
  3. Is the increase one step or a five-year schedule? (Here it is a schedule through Feb 2030.)
