Ordinances and resolutions, 2024 to 2026

On this page (4 sections)
  1. Ordinances
  2. Resolutions
  3. How to check one yourself
  4. Sources cited on this page

An ordinance is a local law; it usually needs two readings. A resolution is a decision or policy the Council adopts in one vote. Here is every one we found, in plain words.

Key takeaways

  • The city's own lists stop at 2025 (ordinances) and 2022 (resolutions). The rest here comes from council minutes Verified1,2.
  • Newest: Ordinance 282-26 (EV charger permits) had only its first reading, on Oct 21, 2026 Verified3.
  • The number 278-26 was used for two different ordinances Verified4,3.
  • Oct 21, 2026 results are from our video notes and unofficial until minutes Verified5.

Look up fees in the fees tracker. Look up land-use rules in Planning basics.

Gaps and oddities in the ordinance recordNumbers used twice, missing numbers, unclear votes
  • The number 278-26 was used twice. In the city's own minutes, the noise ordinance adopted Aug 19, 2026 and the ADU ordinance adopted Sep 16, 2026 both carry it Verified4,3. Ask the City Clerk which number is final before you cite either one.
  • 281 is missing. We found no Ordinance 281-26 in any posted packet.
  • Unposted 2024 ordinances. 256-24, 261-24 and 264-24 are not on the city's Ordinances page Verified1. We know of 256-24 and 261-24 only from the code's history notes, a third-party copy Unverified6.
  • An unclear vote. For 274-25, the minutes show the introduction vote as "5-1", which can't happen on a five-member council. The adoption vote was 4-0 Verified12.
  • Where the code text lives. The adopted text goes into the Williams Municipal Code. Municode is the official host, but it blocks automated readers. See the Municode note on Reference.

How to check one yourself

Open the "Record" link. Minutes in a packet are approved one meeting later, so the Sep 16 minutes appear in the Oct 21 packet. Most packets are scans, and we read them with text recognition (OCR), so check the page itself before you quote it. This is unofficial education, not legal advice.
