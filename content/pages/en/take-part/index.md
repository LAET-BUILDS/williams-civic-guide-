## Public comment

- **General public comment comes near the start of the agenda** (item D on the October 21, 2026 agenda). It is for items *not* listed as public hearings. To speak on an item that *is* on the agenda, wait until that item comes up [[V:packet-2026-10-21]].
- The agenda asks speakers to use the podium and give their name, whether they live in Williams, and any organization they represent. The mayor may set a time limit depending on how many people want to speak [[V:packet-2026-10-21]].
- **State law:** the Council must give the public a chance to speak on matters within its jurisdiction. It may set reasonable time limits but **may not prohibit criticism** of the City's policies or actions. Speakers who use a translator get twice the time (Gov. Code §54954.3) [[V:brown-54954-3]]. Courts have upheld shorter time limits [[U:fac-comment-limits]].
- The Council generally **cannot act** on something that is not on the agenda, so a comment can raise an issue but usually can't get a vote that night [[U:leginfo-brown]].
- Asking for an item on a future agenda is a **request**, not a right. Meeting procedures are set by Council resolution (WMC §2.04.020, third-party copy) [[V:wmc-gocodebook]].

**Tips:** read the packet a few days ahead; write down the item number; bring a short written copy of your comment for the City Clerk so it is in the record.

<h2 id="brown-act">Brown Act: open meetings</h2>

The [Brown Act](/glossary/#brown-act) is California's open-meetings law [[U:leginfo-brown]]. The California Constitution also says "the meetings of public bodies and the writings of public officials and agencies shall be open to public scrutiny" (art. I, §3(b)(1)) [[V:senate-constitutions]].

### Cure-and-correct demand

If the Council takes an action in a way that breaks certain open-meeting rules, you can ask it to undo the action (Gov. Code §54960.1) [[V:brown-54960-1]]:

1. Send a **written demand** to the Council describing the action and the violation.
2. Deadline: **90 days** after the action, or **30 days** if the problem is that the action was not on a properly posted agenda for a meeting held in open session.
3. The Council has **30 days** to cure or refuse. Silence counts as refusal.
4. You then have **15 days** to file in court.

There are exceptions (for example, substantial compliance, some contracts relied on in good faith, and tax and debt actions) [[V:brown-54960-1]].

**More:** [Brown Act guide](/take-part/brown-act/), section by section.

<h2 id="public-records">Public records</h2>

- Under the [California Public Records Act](/glossary/#cpra), you can ask for City records. The City must tell you **within 10 days** whether it has disclosable records, and can extend that by up to 14 days in unusual circumstances. The 10 days is to *decide*, not necessarily to hand everything over [[U:leginfo-7922-535]].
- If records are wrongly withheld, you can go to court, and a winning requester can recover attorney's fees [[U:leginfo-cpra]].
- **Where to send a request:** the City Clerk, 810 E Street, Williams, [(530) 235-3274](tel:+15302353274) [[V:city-council-page]]. Describe the records as clearly as you can, keep a copy, and note the date you sent it.

**More:** [Public Records Act guide](/take-part/public-records-act/).

## Initiative and referendum

### Referendum: challenge a new ordinance

- A [referendum](/glossary/#referendum) petition must be filed within **30 days** after the ordinance is adopted, signed by **10%** of the city's registered voters. The Council must then repeal the ordinance or put it to a vote (Elections Code §9237) [[V:elec-9237]].
- It works on **ordinances only**, not [resolutions](/glossary/#resolution). Budgets, many fees and many contracts are adopted by resolution [[U:research-notes]].
- Williams development agreements are expressly subject to the 30-day referendum window (WMC §17.09, third-party copy) [[V:wmc-gocodebook]].

### Initiative: propose a new ordinance

- An [initiative](/glossary/#initiative) petition needs signatures of **10%** of registered voters. The Council must adopt the measure, put it on the ballot, or order a report first (Elections Code §9215) [[V:elec-9215]].
- The exact number of signatures depends on the current registered-voter count; ask Colusa County Elections [[U:county-elections]].
- The California Constitution lets city voters use initiative and referendum "under procedures that the Legislature shall provide" (art. II, §11) [[V:senate-constitutions]].
