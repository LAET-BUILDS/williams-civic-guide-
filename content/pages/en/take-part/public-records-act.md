# California Public Records Act (CPRA)

**Official text:** Government Code §7920.000 and following [[U:leginfo-cpra]]. Start at [§7920.000 on leginfo.legislature.ca.gov](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=GOV&sectionNum=7920.000). The official site blocked automated downloads when we built this page, so the full text is **not** reproduced here; open the link in a normal browser.

The California Constitution says the people have "the right of access to information concerning the conduct of the people's business" (Art. I, §3(b)(1)) [[V:senate-constitutions]]. [Read Article I, Section 3](/documents/ca-constitution/article-i/#article-i-sec-3).

## Unofficial plain-language summary

<p><span class="tag tag-summary">Unofficial summary</span> Not legal advice; the statute controls.</p>

| Topic | Section | What it says (summary) | Label |
|---|---|---|---|
| Response time | §7922.535 | The agency must decide within 10 days whether it has disclosable records and tell you; it may extend by up to 14 days in unusual circumstances. | [[U:leginfo-7922-535]] |
| Partial release | §7922.525 | Release the parts that are not exempt. | [[U:research-notes]] |
| Help with your request | §7922.600 | The agency should help you identify records and narrow a request. | [[U:research-notes]] |
| Court enforcement | §7923.000, §7923.100 | You can sue to enforce the Act; a successful requester can get court costs and attorney's fees. | [[U:leginfo-cpra]] |

Every row is unverified: check each section on leginfo before you cite it in a request.
