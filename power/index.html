How power works in Williams

On this page (4 sections)
  1. 1. Who holds power and how each gets it
  2. 2. How they use it
  3. 3. How residents push back
  4. Sources cited on this page

On paper, the five-member City Council holds the power in Williams: it passes every ordinance, adopts the budget, approves most contracts, and hires and fires the City Manager and the City Attorney Verified1,2,3,4. In practice, much of the day-to-day leverage sits with those two appointed officials, because they control the staff, the drafts and the legal advice the council relies on when it votes Verified2,5,6. This page separates the two: formal authority (who has the legal right to decide) and practical leverage (who shapes what gets decided). The law gives residents tools aimed at each one.

Unofficial education, not legal advice. Statutes are quoted from unofficial copies; confirm on leginfo before you rely on a deadline.

1. Who holds power and how each gets it

The council has the formal authority; the City Manager and the City Attorney, who answer to it, hold much of the practical leverage. Williams is a general law city, so this structure comes from the California Government Code, filled in by the Williams Municipal Code (WMC) Verified7,8. See the three layers of law.

The council: formal authority, decided by three votes

  • Five members, elected citywide. The council has five members who serve four-year terms and are elected at large Verified9. State law requires "a city council of at least five members" (Gov. Code §36501(a)) Verified10. Three council seats and the City Treasurer are on the November 3, 2026 ballot Verified11.
  • Three votes decide. "A majority of the council constitutes a quorum for transaction of business" (Gov. Code §36810) Verified12. "Resolutions, orders for the payment of money, and all ordinances require a recorded majority vote of the total membership of the city council" (§36936) Verified1. With five members, that is three yes votes, even if only three members attend Calculated1,9. An urgency ordinance needs a four-fifths vote (§36937(b)), which is four of five Calculated13,9.
  • The mayor is a council member with one vote. The council chooses "one of its number as mayor, and one of its number as mayor pro tempore" (Gov. Code §36801) Verified14; in Williams it does so at the first meeting of the year Verified9. The mayor presides at meetings (§36802) Verified15, signs most city contracts after council approval (WMC §2.06.100) Verified2, and appoints planning commissioners "with the approval of the city council" (WMC §2.24.020) Verified16. Mayor Dunlap and Mayor Pro Tem Belmontes Leyva hold those roles in 2026 Verified17.
  • The council hires and fires the City Manager. The office is appointed by the council and held "at the pleasure of the city council" (WMC §2.06.010); the manager "may be removed from office by the city council at any time, with or without cause, and for any reason" (§2.06.090) Verified2. The 2026 contract repeats it: the manager "serves at the will and pleasure of the City Council and may be terminated with or without cause" (Employment Agreement §6.A), and "is subject to the direction and oversight of the City Council" (§1) Verified18.
  • The council alone hires and fires the City Attorney. The council "may appoint a city attorney" (Gov. Code §36505), and appointive officers "hold office during the pleasure of the city council" (§36506) Verified3,4. The City Manager can appoint and dismiss staff "except the city attorney" (Gov. Code §34856; WMC §2.06.050) Verified19,2.
  • The council is paid a stipend. Council members receive $420 a month and the mayor $560 Verified9. The City Manager is "a full-time, exempt employee" Verified18 with a base salary of $175,000, raised to $178,500 by the October 2026 amendment Verified6.

The City Manager: practical leverage over staff, budget and paper

The WMC calls the office "city administrator"; the 2026 employment agreement uses the title City Manager and delegates "all of the duties, powers, and responsibilities of City Manager as set forth in Section 2.06" Verified2,18. Yvonne Kimball has held it since February 19, 2026 Verified18.

  • Controls the staff. The manager is "the administrative head of the government of the city under the direction and control of the city council" (WMC §2.06.040) Verified2, with power "to control, supervise, order and give directions to all department heads" (§2.06.040(B)) and "to order, control, promote, demote, discipline and discharge any and all officers and employees of the city, but excepting elective officers and the city attorney" (§2.06.040(C)) Verified2. The council appoints the finance officer, but that officer works "under the direction of the city administrator" (WMC §2.18.020, §2.18.040) Verified20. The manager also performs "the duties and responsibilities of city planning director" unless the council delegates them elsewhere (§2.06.040(O)) Verified2.
  • Drafts the budget. The manager drafts "a proposed annual budget and salary plan" (WMC §2.06.040(G)) and submits the proposed annual budget by May 1 (§3.20.020) Verified2,5, and "may require any department head or employee to furnish whatever information is deemed necessary" (§3.20.030) Verified5. The council holds hearings, makes changes, and adopts the final budget by June 30; the manager then submits the revised budget "based on the changes approved or directed by the council" (§3.20.040, §3.20.060) Verified5.
  • Decides what gets written up. The manager recommends "the adoption of such ordinances and resolutions as deemed necessary and appropriate" (§2.06.040(E)) Verified2. In the October 21, 2026 packet, staff reports are "Submitted by" or "Reviewed by: Yvonne Kimball, City Manager" Verified6.
  • Shapes the agenda, under rules we haven't found. The WMC leaves meeting "procedures" to a council resolution (§2.04.020) Verified21, and the clerk posts the agendas (§2.08.010(c)) Verified22. We have not found that resolution or any posted rule on who decides what goes on the agenda in Williams Unverified21. State law does let the council, at any meeting, "direct staff to place a matter of business on a future agenda" (Gov. Code §54954.2(a)(3)) Verified23.
  • Carries out decisions and signs smaller contracts. The manager enforces city laws and makes sure contracts "are faithfully observed" (§2.06.040(A)) Verified2 and is the purchasing officer (WMC §3.28.030) Verified24. Contracts of $60,000 or less "may be awarded by the city administrator, upon recommendation of the director of the department … by any alternative procedure" (§3.28.050(a)-3.28.080(a)) Verified24. More than $60,000 goes to the council, with formal bidding above $200,000 (§3.28.050(b)-(c)) Verified24. Change orders count toward the limits, splitting a job "for the purpose of avoiding" them is banned (§3.28.050(g)-(h)), and repeat professional-services awards to one vendor of more than $60,000 in a fiscal year need the council (§3.28.070(g)) Verified24. The manager may declare a public works emergency and spend up to $125,000 before council approval, reporting at the next regular meeting for work over $30,000 (§3.28.130(a)) Verified24.
  • Speaks for the city. The manager negotiates with employee bargaining units and represents the city with the state and other agencies (§2.06.040(I)-(J)) Verified2.

The City Attorney: practical leverage over what counts as legal

The city does not employ its own attorney. The job is contracted to the firm Best Best & Krieger, and partner Andreas Booher serves as City Attorney Verified6,25. We did not find a WMC chapter listing the City Attorney's duties, and we have not reviewed the firm's contract with the city Unverified26.

  • Frames what is legal. At the August 19, 2026 meeting, a council member asked about Senate Bill 707; the minutes record that Mr. Booher "stated that staff and himself had looked at that and SB 707 does not apply to the City of Williams," and he suggested that a request to translate agendas into Spanish come back as a discussion item Verified17. Legal advice like this decides which options reach a vote, and the minutes do not show the reasoning behind it Verified17.
  • Drafts and approves documents. Resolutions and contracts in the packet carry an "APPROVED AS TO FORM" line for "Andreas Booher, City Attorney" Verified6, and city bid and contract documents must be approved by the department director "as well as the city attorney" (WMC §3.28.090-3.28.120) Verified24. At the same meeting, the mayor thanked him "for putting together" the city's response to the 2025-2026 Grand Jury report, which the council approved 4-1 Verified17.
  • Advises in closed session and negotiates. For the September 16, 2026 closed session on the City Manager's contract, the agenda named "Agency Designated Representative: Andreas Booher, City Attorney" (Gov. Code §54957.6) Verified17. What is said in closed session is generally confidential (Gov. Code §54963) Verified27.
  • Holds discretion the code gives him directly. Violations of the WMC are "either a misdemeanor or an infraction, at the discretion of the city attorney" (WMC §1.12.010), with no written criteria Verified28. A misdemeanor can carry a fine up to $1,000 and six months in jail (Gov. Code §36901) Verified29. The water code says the city attorney "is hereby authorized and directed" to start court proceedings against property whose water bill is unpaid 60 days (WMC §13.04.221(F)) Verified30. See Who decides?.
  • Answers to the council. None of this is beyond the council's reach: the council can replace the City Attorney at its pleasure (§36506) Verified4, and it can amend any WMC section by ordinance (§36936) Verified1.

Other offices and boards

  • City Clerk. Keeps the minutes, ordinances, resolutions and records, and posts agendas and notices (WMC §2.08.010) Verified22; also the Form 700 filing officer Verified31. State law lists the clerk and treasurer as elective (Gov. Code §36501), but voters may make them appointive (§36508) Verified10,32. The 2026 ballot has a Treasurer race and no Clerk race Verified11; we have not found the measure that made the Clerk appointive Unverified11.
  • Treasurer and finance officer. The elected Treasurer's financial and accounting duties are transferred "to the city finance officer under the direction of the city administrator" (WMC §2.12.020) Verified33. The council appoints the finance officer (§2.18.020) Verified20.
  • Planning Commission. Five unpaid members, appointed by the mayor with council approval, who serve "at the pleasure of the city council" (WMC §2.24.020-.030, .070) Verified16. Each planning commission "shall report directly to the legislative body" (Gov. Code §65101(a)) Verified34. Its secretary is the planning director (§2.24.050) Verified16, whose duties fall to the City Manager by default (§2.06.040(O)) Verified2. It meets the fourth Monday at 6 p.m. Verified35.
  • Parks and Recreation Commission. Five members, some nominated by the school board (WMC §2.40) Verified36; meets the first Thursday at 6 p.m. Verified35.

Above the city: state and county

State law sets the city's structure, voting rules and conflict rules, and the city can't override them Verified8,7. The county runs voter registration counts that set petition thresholds Verified37, receives the adopted budget (WMC §3.20.060) Verified5, and its civil grand jury "may investigate and report upon the operations, accounts, and records" of a city (Penal Code §925a) Verified38.

2. How they use it

Most decisions reach the council as a written staff recommendation on a posted agenda. These are the steps where formal authority and practical leverage meet Verified6.

The agenda and the staff report

  • 72 hours. Regular meeting agendas must be posted at least 72 hours ahead, and "no action or discussion shall be undertaken on any item not appearing on the posted agenda" (Gov. Code §54954.2) Verified23. Agenda materials given to the council are public records (§54957.5) Verified39.
  • The staff report. Action items in the October 21, 2026 packet each have a report with a background, a fiscal impact and a recommendation, usually naming who prepared, submitted or reviewed it Verified6.

The consent calendar

The Williams agenda says consent items "are routine in nature and will be approved by a single action unless an individual Council Member or member of the public requests an item be removed from the consent calendar for special consideration" Verified6. Minutes of past meetings are approved this way Verified17.

Contracts and spending limits

Most contracts need council approval and the mayor's signature, "except as otherwise authorized by city ordinance" (WMC §2.06.100) Verified2. The purchasing chapter is that ordinance: $60,000 or less can be awarded by the manager; more than $60,000 goes to the council; more than $200,000 needs formal bidding and council-adopted plans (WMC §3.28.050-3.28.080) Verified24. A purchase order needs an "unencumbered appropriation" in the budget unless there is an emergency or council authorization (§3.28.040(e)) Verified24.

Budgets

The manager drafts; the council holds hearings, changes and adopts by June 30, with a mid-year review six months into the fiscal year (WMC §3.20.010-3.20.060) Verified5. The proposed budget comes with a resolution "fixing the compensation of employees and elected officers" (§3.20.050) Verified5.

Ordinances and resolutions

  • Ordinances are the city's laws. They can't be passed within five days of introduction or at a special meeting, except urgency ordinances; a reading can be waived (Gov. Code §36934) Verified40. They "take effect 30 days after their final passage," except urgency (four-fifths vote), election, street and tax ordinances (§36937) Verified13. That 30-day window is when a referendum can be filed (Elec. Code §9235, §9237) Verified41,42.
  • Resolutions also need three votes (§36936) Verified1, but a referendum can't challenge them; it works on ordinances only Unverified26. Budgets, many fees and many contracts are adopted by resolution Unverified26. Williams fine schedules are "established by resolution of the city council" (WMC §8.16.490(A)), not printed in the code Verified43; see Who decides? #7. Property-related fees, such as water rates, still need the Proposition 218 notice and protest process (Cal. Const. Art. XIII D, §6) Verified44.

Closed sessions

Closed sessions are allowed only for listed topics, such as litigation, real estate, some personnel matters and labor negotiations Verified45,46,47,48, and some actions must be reported out (Gov. Code §54957.1) Verified49. See Can the council meet behind closed doors?.

Conflicts of interest

  • Contracts. City officers and employees "shall not be financially interested in any contract made by them in their official capacity, or by any body or board of which they are members" (Gov. Code §1090(a)) Verified50. A contract made in violation may be voided (§1092) Verified51, and a willful violation is a crime that bars the person from office for life (§1097) Verified52.
  • Decisions. An official may not "make, participate in making, or in any way attempt to use the public official's official position to influence a governmental decision" in which the official has a financial interest (Gov. Code §87100) Verified53.
  • Form 700. Mayors, city managers, city attorneys, city treasurers, council members and planning commissioners must disclose their economic interests (Gov. Code §87200) Verified54; other designated employees file under the city's conflict code (§87302) Verified55. A Williams planning commissioner who doesn't file is automatically removed (WMC §2.24.030(D)) Verified16.

Illustration: the City Manager's contract amendment, October 21, 2026

  • Who drafted it. The staff report says that after the City Manager's August performance review, "the City Council directed the City Attorney to negotiate an amendment" Verified6. The September 16 closed session named the City Attorney as the city's negotiator Verified17. The report lists no preparer, and its recommendation reads: "Staff and the City Attorney recommend the City Council review, discuss, and consider adoption" Verified6.
  • What it changed. The term runs five years, to February 18, 2031. The base salary goes from $175,000 to $178,500, a $500-a-month vehicle allowance is added, and severance rises "from three months to six months, depending on longevity." The stated fiscal impact is $6,500 a year Verified6.
  • The vote. It was approved 4-1 on a motion by Mayor Dunlap, seconded by Council Member Belmontes Leyva, with Council Member Troughton voting no Verified56. Council Member Parsons' answer is inaudible on the recording Unverified56. The vote is taken from the meeting video (01:40:32-01:40:48) and is unofficial until the council approves the minutes Verified56.
  • How it shows the structure. The council set the direction and cast the vote. The City Attorney is the one officer the manager cannot appoint or dismiss (WMC §2.06.050), and he handled the negotiation Verified2,6. Staff work under the manager's direction (§2.06.040(B)) Verified2. Severance is capped by state law at no more than the months left on the contract, and never more than 18 months' pay (Gov. Code §53260) Verified57. More: Agenda Watch, October 21.

3. How residents push back

Residents don't vote at the council table, but state law gives them tools at every step: to see what staff and the attorney see, to be heard before a vote, to undo actions taken unlawfully, and to change the council itself. Each tool below goes after either the formal power or the practical leverage.

Close the information gap

  • Read the packet before the meeting. It is posted with the agenda at least 72 hours ahead Verified23, on the city's agenda page Verified58. Read the fiscal impact and the attachments, not only the recommendation Verified6.
  • Request drafts, contracts and emails. City records are "any writing containing information relating to the conduct of the public's business" (Gov. Code §7920.530(a)) Verified59, including city business on personal accounts (City of San Jose v. Superior Court, 2017) Verified60. Signed contracts and invoices fit that definition Calculated59. The city must answer within 10 days whether it has disclosable records (§7922.535) Verified61. To withhold a record, the city must point to an express exemption or show that "the public interest served by not disclosing the record clearly outweighs the public interest served by disclosure" (§7922.000) Verified62. See How do I get city records?.
  • Check Form 700s. Statements of economic interests are public; "No conditions whatsoever shall be imposed" on inspecting them, and copies cost no more than 10 cents a page (Gov. Code §81008) Verified63. Ask the City Clerk, the filing officer Verified31. Compare them with the vendors and properties on the agenda Verified53,50.

Be heard before the vote

  • Public comment. You can speak on any agenda item before or during its consideration, and on other city business in general comment (Gov. Code §54954.3) Verified64. See Can I speak? and Take part: public comment.
  • Written comment. We have not found a posted Williams rule on how written comments reach the council or whether they are read aloud; ask the City Clerk before the meeting Unverified31. See Can I speak?.
  • Pull a consent item. A council member "or member of the public" can ask to remove an item from the consent calendar for separate discussion Verified6.
  • Ask the council to direct staff. The manager works "under the direction and control of the city council" (WMC §2.06.040) Verified2, and the contract counts "unreasonable refusal or failure to act in accordance with any directive or order of the City Council" as cause for termination Verified18. Ask a council member to request a report or an agenda item; the council can "direct staff to place a matter of business on a future agenda" (§54954.2(a)(3)) Verified23. It takes three votes to give direction as a body Calculated1,9.

Undo actions taken the wrong way

  • Cure and correct (§54960.1). Send a written demand within 90 days of the action (30 days if it was not on a properly posted agenda); the council has 30 days to cure or refuse, and then you have 15 days to sue Verified65.
  • Cease and desist (§54960.2). To challenge a past practice, send a letter within nine months; if the council doesn't give an "unconditional commitment" within 30 days, you have 60 days to sue Verified66. See Take part: Brown Act and the Brown Act guide.
  • Proposition 218 protest. For property-related fees like water rates, a majority written protest stops the increase (Cal. Const. Art. XIII D, §6(a)(2)) Verified44. See Can the city raise my water rate?.
  • Conflicts. A contract made in violation of §1090 may be voided, and a suit must be filed within four years of discovery (§1092) Verified51.

Use the vote directly

Williams had 2,276 registered voters in the Secretary of State's September 4, 2026 report Verified37.

  • Referendum: stop a new ordinance. File within 30 days of the date the ordinance is attested by the clerk, signed by at least 10% of voters (Elec. Code §9237) Verified42: at least 228 at that count Calculated37,42. The ordinance is suspended; the council must repeal it or put it on the ballot (§9241) Verified67. It works on ordinances only, not resolutions Unverified26.
  • Initiative: write your own ordinance. City voters may enact ordinances by initiative (Elec. Code §9200) Verified68. With signatures from 10% of voters, also 228 Calculated37,69, the council must adopt it, put it on the ballot, or order a report first (§9215) Verified70. An initiative ordinance adopted by the voters, or adopted by the council without a vote, can't be "repealed or amended except by a vote of the people," unless the ordinance itself says otherwise (§9217) Verified71.
  • Recall. Elective city officers can be recalled (Elec. Code §11000) Verified72; in Williams that means council members and the elected Treasurer Calculated72,11. In Williams that takes at least 569 signatures, 25%, at the September count Calculated37,73. The City Manager and City Attorney are appointed, not elected, so the route to them runs through the council Calculated72,2,3.
  • Elections and commissions. Three council seats and the Treasurer are on the November 3, 2026 ballot Verified11. Planning commissioners must be voters in the city and are appointed by the mayor with council approval (WMC §2.24.020, §2.24.060) Verified16.

The signature counts change with each registration report; confirm the current number with Colusa County Elections before circulating Unverified74.

Outside oversight

  • Fair Political Practices Commission. On "the sworn complaint of any person," the FPPC investigates possible violations of the Political Reform Act (including §87100 conflicts and Form 700 filings), and must tell you within 14 days what it will do (Gov. Code §83115) Verified75. The district attorney is the civil prosecutor for city agencies (§91001) Verified76.
  • The civil grand jury. It may examine a city's books and records and report on its operations (Penal Code §925a) Verified38; the court's grand jury page has a complaint form Verified77. The 2025-2026 Colusa County Civil Grand Jury reported on Williams Verified78:
    • Finding F11 says the council "failed in its fundamental duty to supervise executive leadership and protect the public interest" Verified78. The city "disagrees wholly," answering that the council "exercises oversight of the City Manager (formerly referred to as City Administrator) and City Attorney" Verified79.
    • Finding F6 says the City Manager "instructed the finance staff not to communicate with a city official" Verified78. The city "disagrees partially," saying the former City Administrator issued a memo making himself "the first point of contact for all communications between staff and City Council members" Verified79.
    • Recommendation R1 calls for annual council training on "roles, oversight duties, and compliance with the Brown Act" Verified78. The city's answer, approved 4-1 on August 19, 2026 with Council Member Troughton voting no, says R1 "will not be implemented because it is not warranted or is not reasonable" Verified79,17.
    • The report invited the City Manager and City Attorney to respond within 90 days Verified78. The court's page lists responses from the council and from Council Member Troughton, but none from either official, as of October 29, 2026 Verified77.
