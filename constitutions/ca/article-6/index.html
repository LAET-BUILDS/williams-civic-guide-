California Article VI: The Judiciary

Lesson 63 of 68 · California's state government · 13 min read

Every dispute with the City of Williams that reaches court starts in a superior court created by Article VI Verified1.

Key takeaways

  • Three levels: Supreme Court, courts of appeal, superior courts (§1).
  • Each county has one superior court with an appellate division (§4); municipal courts were unified into them under 1998 amendments (§23).
  • Superior courts hear writs of mandate (§10). That's the usual way to challenge a city decision.
  • No judgment is reversed for error unless it caused "a miscarriage of justice" (§13).

Section 10: Original jurisdiction and writs

Courts "have original jurisdiction in proceedings for extraordinary relief in the nature of mandamus, certiorari, and prohibition," and "Superior courts have original jurisdiction in all other causes" Verified1.

Our reading: a writ of mandate is how residents ask a court to order a city to follow the law, for example after a hearing decision. Inverse condemnation claims are often joined with administrative mandamus (Code Civ. Proc. §1094.5), as Hensler describes (Hensler v. City of Glendale (1994) 8 Cal.4th 1) Verified2.

Section 13: Harmless error

A judgment won't be set aside "unless, after an examination of the entire cause, including the evidence, the court shall be of the opinion that the error complained of has resulted in a miscarriage of justice" Verified1.

Section 14: Written opinions

Sections 15–18: Judges

  • Qualification. 10 years as a State Bar member or judge (§15) Verified1.
  • Terms. Supreme Court and appellate judges serve "12 years" (§16(a)) Verified1.
  • Discipline by the Commission on Judicial Performance (§18) Verified1.
  • Temporary judges by stipulation (§21) Verified1.

In Williams

  • Infractions and misdemeanors under the Williams Municipal Code are prosecuted in the Colusa County Superior Court Unverified3; appeals in limited cases go to its appellate division (§4, §11(b)) Verified1.
  • Challenging a city decision usually means a petition for writ of mandate in superior court (§10) Verified1. Deadlines are short; get advice.

Common misconceptions

  • "Any mistake at trial means a new trial." Only if it caused a miscarriage of justice (§13) Verified1.
  • "Municipal court handles city tickets." Municipal courts were unified into superior courts (§23) Verified1.

Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then open each question.

How many votes does the California Supreme Court need for a judgment?

Four (§2) Verified1.

Which courts can issue writs of mandate?

The Supreme Court, courts of appeal and superior courts (§10) Verified1.

When can an appellate court reverse for an error?

Only if the error "resulted in a miscarriage of justice" (§13) Verified1.
