California Article XIII B: The spending limit and state mandates

Lesson 25 of 68 · Taxes, budgets, fees and utilities · 13 min read

On this page (8 sections)

Article XIII B caps how much tax money each government, Williams included, can spend each year. It also makes the State pay when it orders cities to do more.

Key takeaways

  • Each government's tax-funded appropriations can't exceed last year's limit, adjusted "for the change in the cost of living and the change in population" (§1).
  • User fees count as "proceeds of taxes" only to the extent they exceed reasonable costs (§8(c)).
  • When the State "mandates a new program or higher level of service on any local government," it must reimburse the costs (§6(a)), with exceptions.

Section 6: State mandates

What's a "higher level of service." County of Los Angeles v. State (1987) addressed when state laws imposing costs on local governments trigger the subvention requirement (County of Los Angeles v. State of California (1987) 43 Cal.3d 46) Verified2. Our reading: §6 is aimed at programs the State shifts to local governments, not every state law that happens to cost a city money.

No subvention required for mandates the local agency requested, laws "defining a new crime or changing an existing definition of a crime," and pre-1975 mandates (§6(a)(1)–(3)) Verified1.

Section 8: Definitions

"Proceeds of taxes" include fees "to the extent that those proceeds exceed the costs reasonably borne by that entity in providing the regulation, product, or service" (§8(c)) Verified1.

In Williams

  • The city's annual appropriations limit applies to Williams as a "Local government" under §8(d) Verified1. Our reading: the council adopts the limit as part of budget work; ask the city clerk for the resolution Verified3.
  • New state duties. If Sacramento orders Williams to provide a new program, §6 may require reimbursement Verified1.

Check yourself

Pick an answer for each question. You will see right away if it is right, and why.

Question 1. What two factors adjust the appropriations limit each year?

Show the answer and why

The change in the cost of living and the change in population (§1) Verified1.

Question 2. When must the State reimburse a city for a mandate?

Show the answer and why

When it "mandates a new program or higher level of service," unless an exception applies (§6(a)) Verified1.

Question 3. Are user fees "proceeds of taxes"?

Show the answer and why

Only to the extent they exceed reasonable costs (§8(c)) Verified1.

Question 4. Scenario: Suppose Sacramento orders Williams to start a new program. Who may have to pay?

Show the answer and why

Answer: The state; §6 may require reimbursement.
