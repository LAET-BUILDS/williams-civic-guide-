California Article XIII C: Voter approval for local taxes (Proposition 218)

Lesson 22 of 68 · Taxes, budgets, fees and utilities · 11 min read

On this page (7 sections)
  1. Section 1: Definitions
  2. Section 2: The votes
  3. Section 3: Initiative to cut taxes and fees
  4. In Williams

If Williams calls something a "fee" but it's really a tax, Article XIII C requires a vote. The city carries the burden of proving it isn't a tax Verified1.

Key takeaways

  • Every local tax is "either general taxes or special taxes" (§2(a)).
  • General tax: majority vote, at a regular council election unless there's a unanimous emergency declaration (§2(b)). Special tax: two-thirds (§2(d)).
  • "Tax" means "any levy, charge, or exaction of any kind imposed by a local government," minus seven exceptions (§1(e)).
  • Fines and penalties for violating the law are one exception (§1(e)(5)).
  • Voters may always use the initiative to reduce or repeal local taxes, fees and charges (§3).

Section 1: Definitions

What counts as a tax

Exception (§1(e)) Plain meaning Williams example (our reading)
(1) Specific benefit or privilege Charge for something only the payer gets, at cost A special-event permit
(2) Specific service or product Charge for a service only the payer gets, at cost A copy or document fee
(3) Regulatory costs Cost of licenses, permits, inspections, enforcement Building permit or inspection fees
(4) Use of government property Entrance, rental, lease or purchase Park facility rental
(5) Fines and penalties "A fine, penalty, or other monetary charge imposed by the judicial branch of government or a local government, as a result of a violation of law" Administrative citations
(6) Development conditions "A charge imposed as a condition of property development" Impact fees (also under the Mitigation Fee Act)
(7) XIII D charges Assessments and property-related fees under Article XIII D Water, sewer, refuse charges

Table source: Verified1,2

The burden is on the city. "The local government bears the burden of proving by a preponderance of the evidence that a levy, charge, or other exaction is not a tax, that the amount is no more than necessary to cover the reasonable costs of the governmental activity," and that costs are allocated fairly Verified1.

Before 2010. Sinclair Paint (1997) upheld state charges as "bona fide regulatory fees, not taxes, because the Legislature imposed the fees to mitigate the actual or anticipated adverse effects of the fee payers' operations" (Sinclair Paint Co. v. State Bd. of Equalization (1997) 15 Cal.4th 866) Verified3. The 2010 amendment to §1 wrote a stricter, enumerated test into the constitution Verified1.

Section 2: The votes

  • General vs. special. "'General tax' means any tax imposed for general governmental purposes"; a "'Special tax' means any tax imposed for specific purposes, including a tax imposed for specific purposes, which is placed into a general fund" (§1(a), (d)) Verified1.
  • Majority. "No local government may impose, extend, or increase any general tax unless and until that tax is submitted to the electorate and approved by a majority vote" (§2(b)) Verified1.
  • Two-thirds. Special taxes need "a two-thirds vote" (§2(d)) Verified1.
  • Citizen initiatives. In California Cannabis Coalition v. City of Upland (2017), the Court held that "the requirement in article XIII C, section 2, subdivision (b) — mandating that general taxes be submitted to the voters at a regularly scheduled general election — applies only to local governments and not to the electorate's initiative power" (California Cannabis Coalition v. City of Upland (2017) 3 Cal.5th 924) Verified4. It decided election timing, not whether a citizen-proposed special tax needs two-thirds; we haven't verified the later cases on that question Unverified5.

Section 3: Initiative to cut taxes and fees

The Supreme Court held this "expressly authorizes initiative measures like Kelley's insofar as they seek to reduce or repeal a public agency's water rates and other water delivery charges," but voters "did not intend to authorize initiative measures imposing voter-approval requirements on future water delivery charge increases" (Bighorn-Desert View Water Agency v. Verjil, 2006) Verified6.

In Williams

  • A new city tax goes to voters: majority if general, two-thirds if special Verified1.
  • A "fee" that funds general services may be a tax. Ask the city for the cost study; the city bears the burden Verified1. See Williams: fees.
  • Fines under the WMC are excluded from "tax" (§1(e)(5)), but they're still limited by the Excessive Fines Clauses Verified1,7. See Williams: fines.
  • Repeal by initiative. Residents can propose cutting a local fee or tax, and the signature requirement can't exceed the statewide statutory level (§3) Verified1.

Common misconceptions

  • "Calling it a fee avoids a vote." Not if it fails every §1(e) exception Verified1.
  • "Fines are taxes." No, if imposed "as a result of a violation of law" (§1(e)(5)) Verified1.

Check yourself

Pick an answer for each question. You will see right away if it is right, and why.

Question 1. What vote does a local general tax need?

Show the answer and why

A majority of voters (§2(b)) Verified1.

Question 2. Who must prove that a charge is not a tax?

Show the answer and why

The local government, by a preponderance of the evidence (§1(e)) Verified1.

Question 3. Name three exceptions to "tax."

Show the answer and why

Any three of: specific benefit, specific service, regulatory costs, use of government property, fines and penalties, development conditions, XIII D charges (§1(e)) Verified1.

Question 4. Can voters use an initiative to reduce water rates?

Show the answer and why

Yes (Bighorn), though not to impose voter approval on future increases Verified6.

Question 5. Spot the problem: What's wrong with this sample item?

Show the answer and why

Answer: A general tax needs a majority vote of the voters, not just the council.

Under Article XIII C, a local general tax needs a majority of voters, at a regular council election unless there's a unanimous emergency declaration (§2(b)).
