California Article XIII D: Assessments and property-related fees (Proposition 218)

Lesson 23 of 68 · Taxes, budgets, fees and utilities · 19 min read

Your Williams water, sewer and trash bills are "property-related fees" if they're charged as an incident of owning or occupying property. Article XIII D, §6 sets the rules for how they're adopted and how high they can go Verified1.

Key takeaways

  • Only four levies can be charged to a parcel: the 1% property tax, two-thirds special taxes, XIII D assessments, and XIII D fees (§3).
  • Assessments need a special benefit, an engineer's report, and a mailed ballot weighted by cost; a majority protest kills them (§4).
  • New or higher property-related fees need mailed notice, a hearing at least 45 days later, and no majority written protest (§6(a)).
  • Fee revenue can't exceed the cost of service, can't be diverted, and can't exceed each parcel's proportional cost (§6(b)).
  • Water, sewer and refuse fees skip the separate election; others need one (§6(c)).
  • In court, "the burden shall be on the agency" (§6(b)(5)).

Sections 1–2: Scope and definitions

  • Fee or charge: a levy "imposed by an agency upon a parcel or upon a person as an incident of property ownership, including a user fee or charge for a property-related service" (§2(e)) Verified1.
  • Special benefit: "a particular and distinct benefit over and above general benefits"; "General enhancement of property value does not constitute 'special benefit'" (§2(i)) Verified1.
  • Tenants count where they're directly liable for the charge (§2(g)) Verified1.

Section 3: Only four parcel levies

Section 4: Assessments

  • "Only special benefits are assessable, and an agency shall separate the general benefits from the special benefits conferred on a parcel" (§4(a)) Verified1.
  • "All assessments shall be supported by a detailed engineer's report prepared by a registered professional engineer certified by the State of California" (§4(b)) Verified1.
  • "A majority protest exists if, upon the conclusion of the hearing, ballots submitted in opposition to the assessment exceed the ballots submitted in favor of the assessment" (§4(e)), weighted by financial obligation Verified1.
  • Courts review independently. "Proposition 218 requires courts to make an independent review of local agency decisions that are governed by express constitutional provisions" (Silicon Valley Taxpayers' Assn., Inc. v. Santa Clara County Open Space Authority (2008) 44 Cal.4th 431) Verified2.

Section 5: Grandfathered assessments

Section 6: Property-related fees

The procedure (§6(a))

  1. Identify the parcels and calculate each one's fee.
  2. Mail written notice to each record owner with the amount, the basis, the reason, and the hearing date.
  3. Hold a public hearing "not less than 45 days after mailing the notice."
  4. "If written protests against the proposed fee or charge are presented by a majority of owners of the identified parcels, the agency shall not impose the fee or charge."

Source: Verified1

The substantive limits (§6(b))

  • "Revenues derived from the fee or charge shall not exceed the funds required to provide the property-related service."
  • Revenues "shall not be used for any purpose other than that for which the fee or charge was imposed."
  • The fee "shall not exceed the proportional cost of the service attributable to the parcel."
  • "No fee or charge may be imposed for a service unless that service is actually used by, or immediately available to, the owner of the property in question. Fees or charges based on potential or future use of a service are not permitted."
  • "No fee or charge may be imposed for general governmental services including, but not limited to, police, fire, ambulance or library services, where the service is available to the public at large in substantially the same manner as it is to property owners."
  • "In any legal action contesting the validity of a fee or charge, the burden shall be on the agency to demonstrate compliance with this article."

Source: Verified1

The vote (§6(c))

"Except for fees or charges for sewer, water, and refuse collection services, no propertyrelated fee or charge shall be imposed or increased unless and until that fee or charge is submitted and approved by a majority vote of the property owners ... or, at the option of the agency, by a two-thirds vote of the electorate residing in the affected area" Verified1.

How courts read it

Case Holding (quoted)
Apartment Assn. v. Los Angeles (2001) Asked "whether a city ordinance imposing an inspection fee on private landlords violates article XIII D" and answered: "We conclude that it does not" (Apartment Assn. of Los Angeles County, Inc. v. City of Los Angeles (2001) 24 Cal.4th 830) Verified3
Richmond v. Shasta CSD (2004) "a capacity charge imposed as a condition for making a new connection to a water system ... is not an assessment within the meaning of article XIII D" (Richmond v. Shasta Community Services Dist. (2004) 32 Cal.4th 409) Verified4
Bighorn v. Verjil (2006) Voters may reduce water rates by initiative under XIII C §3 Verified5
Capistrano Taxpayers (2015) Tiered water rates are allowed, but "the tiers must still correspond to the actual cost of providing service at a given level of usage" (Capistrano Taxpayers Assn., Inc. v. City of San Juan Capistrano (2015) 235 Cal.App.4th 1493) Verified6
HJTA v. Salinas (2002) A storm drainage fee was a property-related fee needing voter approval; the court noted voters intended the provision "be construed liberally to curb the rise in 'excessive' taxes" (Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Assn. v. City of Salinas (2002) 98 Cal.App.4th 1351) Verified7
HJTA v. Roseville (2002) A 4% "in-lieu franchise fee" on city water, sewer and refuse transferred to the general fund fell within §6: "We conclude the in-lieu fee is within this definition" (Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Assn. v. City of Roseville (2002) 97 Cal.App.4th 637) Verified8

Our reading: Apartment Assn. treated a fee on landlords as a business, not an incident of owning property; fees tied to an activity rather than to ownership may fall outside §6.

In Williams

  • Water and sewer rate increases. Watch for the mailed notice; you have until the end of the hearing to file a written protest. If a majority of parcels protest, the increase fails (§6(a)(2)) Verified1.
  • Ask for the rate study. The city must show revenue doesn't exceed cost and each parcel's share is proportional (§6(b)) Verified1.
  • Transfers to the general fund from utility funds raise Roseville questions Verified8.
  • State statute. The Proposition 218 Omnibus Implementation Act adds procedure (Gov. Code §53750 and following, notice in §53755) Verified9,10.

Common misconceptions

  • "Water rates need a public vote." Not under §6(c); they need the notice, hearing and protest process Verified1.
  • "Any benefit to property justifies an assessment." Only special benefits; "General enhancement of property value does not constitute 'special benefit'" Verified1.

Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then open each question.

How long after mailing notice must the fee hearing be?

At least 45 days (§6(a)(2)) Verified1.

What happens if a majority of parcel owners file written protests?

"The agency shall not impose the fee or charge" (§6(a)(2)) Verified1.

Can a property-related fee pay for police?

Not where the service is available to the public at large in substantially the same manner (§6(b)(5)) Verified1.

Which fees are exempt from the §6(c) election?

Sewer, water and refuse collection Verified1.

Who has the burden in a lawsuit over a fee?

The agency Verified1.
