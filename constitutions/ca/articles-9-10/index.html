California Articles IX, X, X A and X B: Education and water

Lesson 40 of 68 · Alcohol, schools, water and other state rules · 9 min read

On this page (7 sections)

  2. Article X: Water
  3. Article X A: Water Resources Development
  4. Article X B: Marine Resources Protection Act of 1990
  5. In Williams

Article IX covers public schools; Article X covers water. A school district, not the city, runs Williams schools, but the city's water conservation rules lean on Article X.

Key takeaways

  • "The Legislature shall provide for a system of common schools" (IX §5).
  • Education is treated as a fundamental interest under the state equal protection clauses (Serrano, Butt).
  • Water must be put to "beneficial use," and "waste or unreasonable use" is prevented (X §2).
  • The public trust protects navigable waters (National Audubon).

Article IX: Education

Equal education. Serrano v. Priest (1971): the funding scheme "invidiously discriminates against the poor because it makes the quality of a child's education a function of the wealth of his parents and neighbors" (Serrano v. Priest (1971) 5 Cal.3d 584) Verified2. In Butt v. State (1992), the trial court found a district's early closure would deny students their "fundamental right to an effective public education," and the State itself is responsible for "the 'fundamental' educational rights of California students" (Butt v. State of California (1992) 4 Cal.4th 668) Verified3.

Article X: Water

  • Reasonable use. The 1928 amendment was "construed as applying a rule of reasonable use 'to all water rights enjoyed or asserted in this state'" (Joslin v. Marin Municipal Water Dist. (1967) 67 Cal.2d 132) Verified4.
  • Public trust. "We conclude that the public trust doctrine, as recognized and developed in California decisions, protects navigable waters from harm caused by diversion of nonnavigable tributaries" (National Audubon Society v. Superior Court (1983) 33 Cal.3d 419) Verified5.

In Williams

  • Water conservation rules in the WMC rest on the city's police power and Article X, §2's reasonable-use policy Verified1.
  • Schools in Williams are run by a school district under state law, not the city. Our reading: concerns go to the district board, not the council.

Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then open each question.

What does Article X, §2 prohibit?

"Waste or unreasonable use or unreasonable method of use of water" Verified1.

What did National Audubon hold?

The public trust protects navigable waters from harm caused by diverting nonnavigable tributaries Verified5.

Can public money support a sectarian school?

No (IX §8) Verified1.
