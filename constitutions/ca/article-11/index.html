California Article XI: Local government, and why Williams can regulate at all

Lesson 9 of 68 · Local government and the city council · 19 min read

Article XI is the source of every Williams ordinance. Section 7 gives the city its police power. The same sentence limits it: no conflict with general laws Verified1.

Key takeaways

  • Williams "was made a General Law City on May 20, 1920" Verified2. It has no charter, so the "municipal affairs" protection of §5 doesn't apply.
  • §7: a city "may make and enforce within its limits all local, police, sanitary, and other ordinances and regulations not in conflict with general laws."
  • A local law conflicts if it "duplicates, contradicts, or enters an area fully occupied by general law" (Candid, Inland Empire).
  • §9 authorizes a city water system; §10 bars retroactive extra pay; §11 bars handing city functions to private bodies.

Section 1: Counties

Counties are "legal subdivisions of the State" (§1(a)), and §1(b) requires an elected county sheriff and an elected district attorney Verified1. Williams sits in Colusa County Verified3,4.

Section 2: Cities

"The Legislature shall prescribe uniform procedure for city formation and provide for city powers" (§2(a)) Verified1. For a general law city the Government Code does that. "Cities organized under the general law shall be 'general law cities'" (Gov. Code §34102) Verified5. The government of a general law city is vested in a council "of at least five members," a city clerk, a city treasurer, a chief of police, a fire chief and subordinate officers (Gov. Code §36501) Verified6.

Sections 3–6: Charters and municipal affairs

A charter city's charter "with respect to municipal affairs shall supersede all laws inconsistent therewith" (§5(a)) Verified1. Courts call this "home rule": it "reserves to charter cities the right to adopt and enforce ordinances that conflict with general state laws, provided the subject of the regulation is a 'municipal affair'" (Traders Sports, Inc. v. City of San Leandro (2001) 93 Cal.App.4th 37) Verified7. But "state law is supreme with respect to matters of 'statewide concern'" (State Building & Construction Trades Council of California v. City of Vista (2012) 54 Cal.4th 547, citing California Fed. Savings & Loan Assn. v. City of Los Angeles) Verified8.

For Williams: none of this applies until voters adopt a charter by "majority vote of its electors" (§3(a)) Verified1. A general law city has only the powers state law gives it.

Section 7: The police power

What it gives. "This inherent local police power includes broad authority to determine, for purposes of the public health, safety, and welfare, the appropriate uses of land within a local jurisdiction's borders, and preemption by state law is not lightly presumed" (City of Riverside v. Inland Empire Patients Health & Wellness Center, Inc. (2013) 56 Cal.4th 729) Verified9. The Government Code says the same for general law cities: "The legislative body may pass ordinances not in conflict with the Constitution and laws of the State or the United States" (§37100) Verified10.

What limits it: preemption. "A conflict exists if the local legislation 'duplicates, contradicts, or enters an area fully occupied by general law, either expressly or by legislative implication'" (Candid Enterprises, Inc. v. Grossmont Union High School Dist. (1985) 39 Cal.3d 878; City of Riverside v. Inland Empire Patients Health & Wellness Center, Inc. (2013) 56 Cal.4th 729) Verified11,9.

Test Plain meaning Example from the cases
Duplicates Local law repeats state law Typically arises with local criminal laws copying state crimes
Contradicts Local law requires what state law forbids, or forbids what state law requires —
Fully occupied, expressly State says it alone regulates —
Fully occupied, by implication The state scheme is so complete there's no room Big Creek: county timber-location rules not preempted Verified12

Inland Empire (2013) held Riverside's zoning ban on medical marijuana dispensaries was not preempted by state medical marijuana laws Verified9. Big Creek (2006) asked whether county zoning rules on "the permissible locations for timber operations are preempted by state forestry statutes," and answered: "Concluding they are not" (Big Creek Lumber Co. v. County of Santa Cruz (2006) 38 Cal.4th 1139) Verified12.

Our reading: the empty example cells are honest: we list only examples we've verified. Whether a particular Williams ordinance is preempted is a legal question a court decides.

Section 7.5: City measures can't carve out areas

Section 9: City utilities

"A municipal corporation may establish, purchase, and operate public works to furnish its inhabitants with light, water, power, heat, transportation, or means of communication" (§9(a)) Verified1. Charges for water and sewer service fall under Article XIII D, §6 Verified1. See Article XIII D.

Section 10: No extra pay; residency

A city "may not require that its employees be residents of such city," but may require them "to reside within a reasonable and specific distance of their place of employment" (§10(b)) Verified1.

Section 11: No private control of city functions

The Legislature "may not delegate to a private person or body power to ... interfere with county or municipal corporation improvements, money, or property, or to levy taxes or assessments, or perform municipal functions" (§11(a)) Verified1. Our reading: a city may hire contractors, but core government power stays with public bodies.

Sections 12–15: Claims, taxes, vehicle fees

§12 is the basis for claim-filing rules before suing a city Verified1. §15 allocates vehicle license fee revenue to local governments Verified1.

In Williams

  • Every WMC chapter rests on §7 and Gov. Code §37100 Verified1,10. Enforcement rules, such as making a violation a misdemeanor or infraction, come from Gov. Code §36900 Verified13.
  • Preemption challenges. If a WMC section contradicts a state statute, the state wins (§7) Verified1. See worked path.
  • Water service rests on §9 and is limited by XIII D §6 Verified1.
  • Ordinance procedure: five days between introduction and adoption (Gov. Code §36934) and effect 30 days after final passage (§36937) Verified14,15.

Common misconceptions

  • "Cities have home rule." Only charter cities, and only for municipal affairs Verified1,7. Williams is general law Verified2.
  • "If the state has any law on a subject, cities can't touch it." No. Preemption "is not lightly presumed" Verified9.

Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then open each question.

What kind of city is Williams, and since when?

A general law city since May 20, 1920 Verified2.

What are the three ways a local law can conflict with state law?

It "duplicates, contradicts, or enters an area fully occupied by general law" Verified11.

Can Williams require employees to live in the city?

No, but it can require them to live within a reasonable and specific distance (§10(b)) Verified1.

Which section authorizes a city water system?

§9(a) Verified1.
