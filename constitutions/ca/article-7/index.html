California Article VII: Public officers and employees

Lesson 64 of 68 · California's state government · 11 min read

Article VII creates the state civil service. City employees aren't part of it, but one section reaches city elections directly: campaign libel can cost a winner a city office.

Key takeaways

  • State jobs are filled by "a general system based on merit ascertained by competitive examination" (§1(b)).
  • The civil service carries an implied limit on contracting out state work (Professional Engineers, 1997), later eased for architecture and engineering by Article XXII.
  • §10 can cost a candidate a city office for campaign libel found in a civil case.
  • City employees are not in the state civil service; Williams' personnel rules come from statute and its own rules.

Section 1: Civil service

The implied contracting limit. The Court held: "If the constitutional civil service mandate is to retain any vitality as a protective device against the deterioration of the civil service system through private contracting, we must hold that" Caltrans' contracting violated it (Professional Engineers in California Government v. Department of Transportation (1997) 15 Cal.4th 543) Verified2. Voters later added Article XXII to allow contracting for architectural and engineering services Verified1.

Sections 2–6: Board, exemptions, preferences

Section 7: Dual office

Section 8: Bribery and disqualification

"Every person shall be disqualified from holding any office of profit in this State who shall have been convicted of having given or offered a bribe to procure personal election or appointment" (§8(a)) Verified1.

Sections 9–10: Overthrow and campaign libel

§10 covers "any county, city and county, city, district, or any other local elective office" where libel or slander "was a major contributing cause in the defeat of an opposing candidate" Verified1.

In Williams

  • Council elections. A Williams council winner found civilly liable for defamatory campaign statements that were a major cause of an opponent's defeat would lose the seat under §10 Verified1.
  • Bribery disqualifies from office under §8 Verified1. Conflicts of interest are covered by statute, such as Gov. Code §1090 Verified3.

Question 1. How are permanent state civil service jobs filled?

Show the answer and why

By "a general system based on merit ascertained by competitive examination" (§1(b)) Verified1.

Question 2. Does §10's campaign libel rule apply to city offices?

Show the answer and why

Yes; it lists "city" and "any other local elective office" Verified1.

Question 3. What did Professional Engineers (1997) recognize?

Show the answer and why

An implied civil service limit on private contracting for state work Verified2.
