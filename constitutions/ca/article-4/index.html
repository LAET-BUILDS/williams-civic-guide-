California Article IV: The Legislature

Lesson 61 of 68 · California's state government · 24 min read

Williams is a general law city. Its powers come from statutes passed under Article IV Verified1,2. When the Legislature changes the Government Code, the city's rules change.

Key takeaways

  • Legislative power is in the Senate and Assembly, "but the people reserve to themselves the powers of initiative and referendum" (§1).
  • 40 senators, 80 Assembly members; a 12-year lifetime limit (§2).
  • Bills must be in print 72 hours before a final vote (§8(b)(2)), and floor and committee proceedings are open (§7(c)).
  • "A local or special statute is invalid in any case if a general statute can be made applicable" (§16(b)).
  • No extra pay after the fact, for state or city (§17).

Section 1: Legislative power

Section 1.5 and 2: Term limits and the houses

  • 40 and 80. "The Senate has a membership of 40 Senators elected for 4-year terms" and the Assembly "80 members elected for 2-year terms" (§2(a)) Verified1.
  • 12 years. "During her or his lifetime a person may serve no more than 12 years in the Senate, the Assembly, or both" (§2(a)(4)) Verified1. The earlier Proposition 140 limits were upheld: "We conclude that Proposition 140's term limitations extend over the lifetime of each affected officeholder" (Legislature v. Eu (1991) 54 Cal.3d 492) Verified3. The same case held "the pension restrictions of Proposition 140 are invalid as to incumbent legislators" Verified3.
  • Why. §1.5 says "The increased concentration of political power in the hands of incumbent representatives has made our electoral system less free, less competitive, and less representative" Verified1.

Section 7: Open proceedings

"The proceedings of each house and the committees thereof shall be open and public," and that includes "the right of any person to record by audio or video" (§7(c)(1)) Verified1. The Brown Act does the same for Williams' council Verified4. See Article I, §3.

Section 8: How a bill moves

  • "The Legislature may make no law except by statute and may enact no statute except by bill" (§8(b)(1)) Verified1.
  • Urgency statutes take effect immediately but need two-thirds of each house (§8(d)) Verified1.

Section 10: Veto and line-item veto

A bill "becomes a statute if it is signed by the Governor"; a veto can be overridden with "twothirds of the membership concurring" in each house (§10(a)) Verified1. The Governor may also reduce or eliminate items of appropriation (§10(e)) Verified1.

Section 12: The budget

"Within the first 10 days of each calendar year, the Governor shall submit to the Legislature ... a budget" (§12(a)) Verified1. State budget choices reach Williams through local revenue allocations; see Article XIII and Article XIII B.

Section 16: Uniform general laws

"All laws of a general nature have uniform operation" (§16(a)) Verified1. Our reading: this is why most of the Government Code treats every general law city alike, and why special legislation for one city must be justified.

Section 17: No extra compensation

The Legislature can't "authorize a city ... to grant ... extra compensation or extra allowance to a public officer, public employee, or contractor after service has been rendered" (§17) Verified1. Article XI, §10 says the same for local governments directly Verified1. See Article XI.

In Williams

  • City powers are statutes. Williams' authority to adopt ordinances flows from the constitution (Art. XI §7) and the Government Code (e.g., §36900 on enforcing ordinances) Verified1,5. Change the statute, change the power.
  • Bonuses. Our reading: a retroactive bonus to a city employee or contractor for work already done would raise §17 and Art. XI §10 issues Verified1.

Common misconceptions

  • "Term limits were struck down." No. Legislature v. Eu upheld them; only pension limits for incumbents fell Verified3.
  • "Cities pass their own laws independent of Sacramento." General law cities act under state law; see state law and the city.

Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then open each question.

How many years may a person serve in the Legislature?

No more than 12, in either or both houses (§2(a)(4)) Verified1.

What does §16(b) say about special statutes?

"A local or special statute is invalid in any case if a general statute can be made applicable" Verified1.

Can the Legislature authorize a city to pay a contractor extra after the work is done?

No (§17) Verified1.
