Twenty-second Amendment: Presidential term limits

Lesson 53 of 68 · The President, federal courts and the states · 6 min read

On this page (9 sections)

George Washington stepped down after two terms, and Presidents followed that custom until Franklin Roosevelt won four elections. The Twenty-second Amendment made the custom law: no one can be elected President more than twice. California sets its own term limits for state offices. A city council like Williams's has term limits only if the city's voters approve them.

Key takeaways

  • Passed by Congress March 21, 1947; ratified February 27, 1951 Verified1.
  • No one can be elected President more than twice Verified1.
  • Someone who served more than two years of another President's term can be elected only once. The most anyone can serve is ten years Calculated1.
  • "The Twenty-Second Amendment has yet to be applied" by a court, and some questions are still open Verified2.
  • In Williams: under state law, council term limits apply only if city voters approve them at a regularly scheduled election Verified3.

The text

Clause by clause

  • "No person shall be elected to the office of the President more than twice." The limit is on elections, not years in office Verified1.
  • Partial terms. Anyone who "has held the office of President, or acted as President, for more than two years of a term to which some other person was elected President" can be elected only once more Verified1. Our arithmetic: a Vice President who takes over with two years or less left can still be elected twice, for a total of up to ten years Calculated1.
  • Grandfather clause. It didn't apply to the President in office when it was proposed (Harry Truman) Verified1.
  • Seven-year deadline. It had to be ratified within seven years, like the Eighteenth, Twentieth and Twenty-first Verified1.

History

A 1947 House report described "a well-defined custom which has risen in the past that no President should have more than two terms in that office." The amendment was meant "to submit this question to the people" Verified2. That move "followed soon after the people had elected Franklin D. Roosevelt to unprecedented third and fourth terms of office, in 1940 and 1944, respectively" Verified2.

Congress picked its words on purpose. Broader language saying a two-term President "shall be chosen or serve as President ... or be eligible to hold the office" was rejected "in favor of the Twenty-Second Amendment's ban merely on election" Verified2.

Open questions

Because the amendment bars only election, it "would not prevent someone who had twice been elected President from succeeding to the office after having been elected or appointed Vice President" Verified2. The catch is the Twelfth Amendment: "no person constitutionally ineligible to the office of President shall be eligible to that of Vice-President" Verified1. Is a two-term President "constitutionally ineligible"? No court has decided Verified2. See Twelfth Amendment.

Term limits in California

  • Legislators. "During her or his lifetime a person may serve no more than 12 years in the Senate, the Assembly, or both, in any combination of terms" (Art. IV, §2) Verified4. See Article IV.
  • Governor. "No Governor may serve more than 2 terms" (Art. V, §2) Verified4. See Article V.
  • Congress is different. States can't put term limits on their members of Congress. U.S. Term Limits v. Thornton held that state-by-state rules would be "inconsistent with the Framers' vision of a uniform National Legislature" (U.S. Term Limits, Inc. v. Thornton, 514 U.S. 779 (1995)) Verified5.

In Williams

  • No federal limit on council terms. The Twenty-second Amendment covers only the presidency.
  • How a city adopts council term limits. State law lets "the city council of a general law or charter city" adopt, or residents propose "by initiative," a limit on council terms or an elected mayor's terms. A limit "shall apply prospectively only" and needs a majority of voters "at a regularly scheduled election" (Gov. Code §36502(b)) Verified3. Williams is a general law city Verified6.
  • What we couldn't verify. We haven't found a Williams ordinance or ballot measure limiting council terms. Ask the City Clerk before assuming there is or isn't one.
  • Staggered terms. The city's council page lists some members' terms ending November 2026 and others November 2028, so seats come up in different election years Verified7.

Common misconceptions

  • "A President can never serve more than eight years." Someone who finishes two years or less of another's term can still be elected twice Calculated1.
  • "Term limits were in the original Constitution." Two terms was only a custom until 1951 Verified2.
  • "States can term-limit their members of Congress." Thornton says no Verified5.
  • "The council can impose term limits on its own vote." Under §36502(b), voters must approve them Verified3.

Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then open each question.

What is the maximum time someone can serve as President?

Ten years: up to two years of a predecessor's term plus two elected terms Calculated1.

What prompted the amendment?

Franklin Roosevelt's election to third and fourth terms Verified2.

How many years can a person serve in the California Legislature?

No more than 12 in the Senate, Assembly, or both (Art. IV, §2) Verified4.

Can Williams limit council terms?

Yes, but only if city voters approve it at a regularly scheduled election, and it applies only going forward (Gov. Code §36502(b)) Verified3.
