Twenty-sixth Amendment: Voting at 18

Lesson 37 of 68 · U.S. Constitution: Amendments 11–27 · 2 min read

On this page (5 sections)
  1. The text
  2. History
  3. In Williams
  4. Check yourself
  5. Sources cited on this page

The Twenty-sixth Amendment says citizens 18 and older can't be denied the vote "on account of age." It was adopted in about three months after the Supreme Court split on whether Congress could do it by statute.

Key takeaways

  • Passed by Congress March 23, 1971; ratified July 1, 1971. It changed the "twenty-one" in Fourteenth Amendment §2 Verified[1].
  • Covers every election, including city elections.
  • Followed Oregon v. Mitchell (1970), which let Congress set 18 for federal elections but not state and local ones.
  • California's constitution matches: Art. II, §2(a).

The text

Official text — Amendment XXVI, Section 1 · read in context

The right of citizens of the United States, who are eighteen years of age or older, to vote shall not be denied or abridged by the United States or by any State on account of age.

Source: National Archives transcription Verified[1]

Official text — Amendment XXVI, Section 2 · read in context

The Congress shall have power to enforce this article by appropriate legislation.

Source: National Archives transcription Verified[1]

History

Support grew "as Americans questioned why young soldiers fighting in Korea and Vietnam could not exercise the franchise" Verified[2]. Congress "lowered the minimum voting age to 18 in federal elections—but not state elections—by enacting the Voting Rights Act Amendments of 1970" Verified[2]. The 1970 law tried to cover all elections. In Oregon v. Mitchell, the deciding opinion said Congress could "enfranchise 18-year-old citizens in national elections" but "cannot set the voting age in state and local elections" (Oregon v. Mitchell, 400 U.S. 112 (1970)) Verified[3]. The amendment followed within months.

In Williams

Any citizen 18 or older who lives in Williams and is registered can vote in city elections. California: "A United States citizen 18 years of age and resident in this State may vote" (Art. II, §2(a)) Verified[4].

Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then open each question.

What did *Oregon v. Mitchell* decide about the 1970 law?

Congress could set 18 as the voting age for federal elections but not for state and local elections Verified[3].

Does the Twenty-sixth Amendment apply to a Williams city council election?

Yes. It covers the United States and every state, including local elections.
