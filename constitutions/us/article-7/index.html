Article VII: Ratification, and how the Constitution took effect

Lesson 11 of 68 · U.S. Constitution: Preamble and the seven articles · 3 min read

Article VII says how the Constitution itself would become law: ratification by special conventions in nine of the thirteen states. It's the shortest article and finished its work in 1788–89, but it shows something important about the Constitution's authority.

Key takeaways

  • Nine state conventions were enough, not all thirteen as the Articles of Confederation required for amendments.
  • Conventions, not state legislatures, ratified. That tied the Constitution to the people, matching "We the People."
  • The new government began operating in March 1789.

The text

What it says

  • Nine of thirteen. Ratification by conventions of nine states was "sufficient for the Establishment of this Constitution between the States so ratifying the Same." A state that didn't ratify wasn't bound.
  • The note about corrections. The second paragraph lists words that were inserted between lines or written over erasures on the parchment, so no one could later claim the document was altered.
  • The attestation. Signed in Convention on September 17, 1787, attested by the Convention's secretary, William Jackson, followed by the signatures of the delegates Verified1.

History

The Articles of Confederation required every state to agree to any change. The framers went around that rule: they wrote a new constitution and sent it to state conventions elected for that purpose. Using conventions instead of state legislatures meant the Constitution came from the people of each state, the point made in the Preamble and later in McCulloch v. Maryland Verified2.

When did it take effect?

The ninth ratification was reached in 1788. The Supreme Court later explained the timing: Congress under the old Articles set the first Wednesday in March 1789 as the start, and "its operation did not commence before the first Wednesday in March 1789" (Owings v. Speed, 1820, as quoted in the Constitution Annotated) Verified3.

How it reaches Williams

It doesn't today; its job is done. California joined the Union decades after ratification and was bound by the Constitution as a new state under Article IV.

Common misconceptions

  • "All thirteen states had to agree." Nine was enough.
  • "State legislatures ratified the Constitution." Special conventions did.

Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then open each question.

How many state conventions were needed to establish the Constitution?

Nine Verified1.

Why did the framers use conventions instead of state legislatures?

To ground the Constitution in the people of each state, consistent with "We the People" and McCulloch's reading that the government "proceeds directly from the people" Verified2.
