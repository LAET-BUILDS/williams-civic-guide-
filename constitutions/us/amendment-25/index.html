Twenty-fifth Amendment: Presidential succession and disability

Lesson 36 of 68 · U.S. Constitution: Amendments 11–27 · 4 min read

On this page (5 sections)
  1. The text
  2. Section 4 in detail
  3. In Williams
  4. Check yourself
  5. Sources cited on this page

The original Constitution was unclear about whether a Vice President became President or just acted as one, and it had no way to fill a vice presidential vacancy or handle a disabled President. The Twenty-fifth Amendment answers all three.

Key takeaways

  • Passed by Congress July 6, 1965; ratified February 10, 1967 Verified1.
  • §1. If the President is removed, dies or resigns, the Vice President becomes President.
  • §2. A vacant vice presidency is filled by presidential nomination confirmed by majority vote of both houses.
  • §3. The President can voluntarily hand power to the Vice President as Acting President.
  • §4. The Vice President and a majority of the Cabinet can declare the President unable to serve; Congress resolves disputes.

The text

Section 4 in detail

If the President disputes a declaration of inability, the conferees who wrote the final version chose "a four-day deadline for the Vice President (as Acting President) and Cabinet (or other body) to challenge a President's recovery declaration under Section 4; ... a 48-hour deadline for Congress to assemble (if not in session) after the relevant officials challenge the President's recovery declaration; and ... a 21-day deadline for Congress to decide" Verified2. Keeping the President out requires two-thirds of both houses.

In Williams

None directly. For local offices, state law fills vacancies; in a general law city, the council handles a council vacancy by appointment or special election under the Government Code. Our reading: see the Government Code and the city clerk for the exact procedure Verified3.

Check yourself

Answer in your head first, then open each question.

Under §1, does the Vice President "act as" or "become" President when the President dies?

Becomes President.

Who confirms a new Vice President under §2?

A majority vote of both houses of Congress.

How large a vote does Congress need to keep a disputing President out of power under §4?

Two-thirds of both houses.
