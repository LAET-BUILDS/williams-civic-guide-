City Council, Wednesday, October 21, 2026

Regular meeting · 6:00 PM · City Hall Council Chambers, 810 E Street

Held. Minutes not yet approved, so every vote below is unofficial until the Council approves the minutes.

Official agenda & packet (PDF) Meeting video (AV Capture)

About $2.5 million in contracts and truck purchases (maximum authorized amounts, items 12, 13, 15 and 17) Calculated1, a General Fund cash backstop of up to $800,000 for the water fund, automatic yearly fee increases, and a raise, a vehicle allowance and doubled severance for the City Manager Verified1.

Why this meeting matters. Most of the money on this agenda was decided in titles that don't show it. The two biggest examples: the General Fund cash backstop for the water fund (item 15) and the City Manager's severance (item 18) appear only inside the staff reports. If you only read the agenda, you'd miss both. Every vote below is copied from the meeting video, because the Council has not approved minutes for this meeting yet.

Public speakers are not named on this site. City officials and staff are, because they act for the public.

What the flags mean
  • Money Spends, moves, or commits public money
  • Contract Awards, amends, or joins a contract or agreement
  • Staff pay/benefits Changes pay, benefits, or severance for city staff or officials
  • Fees/rates Changes what residents or businesses pay
  • Consent: look closer On the consent calendar (approved in one vote, no discussion) but deserves a look

A flag means “check this one,” not “something is wrong.” Votes are copied from the meeting video until the Council approves minutes; the minutes are the official record.

D. Public comment

D Public comment (items not on the agenda)

Two members of the public spoke. A local business owner asked the City for a permanent fix to a neighborhood problem at the east end of 6th Street (the audio is partly unclear on the exact issue) Unverified2. A Public Works employee briefly said he would speak on item 18 instead Verified2.

The rule behind this: First Amendment: public comment · Cal. Const. Art. I §3: petition and open government

E. Written communications

1 Letter from Recology about its annual rate increase

Fees/rates

Recology, the garbage hauler, notified the City of its yearly rate increase. The Council only "receives" letters like this; there is no vote Verified1.

Look closer: Your garbage bill is going up and it reached the Council as a letter, not a decision. Ask what the franchise agreement lets Recology raise without a vote, and when the Council last reviewed it.

The rule behind this: Art. XIII D §6: notice, hearing, majority protest · Williams: fees and rates

Item text: Verified1

2 El Niño letter from the State Water Resources Control Board and the Governor's emergency proclamation

State notices about El Niño flood and storm readiness. Received for the record; no vote Verified1.

Item text: Verified1

3 Letter of recommendation for the Chief of Jackets at City Hall from the Mayor Pro Tem

A recommendation letter from the Mayor Pro Tem for the Chief of Jackets at City Hall. Received; no vote Verified1.

Item text: Verified1

4 Antidegradation Analysis from PACE Engineering

Money

A consultant study supporting an increase in the wastewater the City is permitted to discharge to Salt Creek, from 0.5 to 0.7 million gallons a day. The packet lists the cost as part of PACE's "existing engineering service contract." Received and filed; no vote Verified1.

Look closer: A real environmental and growth decision arrived as "written communication." Ask what PACE has billed under its existing contract this year, and when the Council will actually discuss the discharge increase.

Item text: Verified1

F. Public hearing

5 First reading of Ordinance 282-26, electric-vehicle charger permit streamlining (new Chapter 15.33)

Creates a fast-track permit process for EV charging stations, which state law (Government Code §65850.7, added in 2015) requires cities to adopt. Staff lists no immediate fiscal impact Verified1. During the hearing staff said it "should have been put in 10 years ago" Verified2. A second reading is needed before it takes effect.

Look closer: This was about a decade late. Ask what other state-required updates the City has not made yet.

Unofficial result, from the meeting video Introduced, first reading waived 4-1 Verified2

motion: Belmontes Leyva, second: Troughton · No: Parsons · Video time 00:12:38–00:13:16

The rule behind this: State and city: ordinances · Art. XI §7: city police power

Item text: Verified1

9 Resolution 26-36, non-voting membership in the California Municipal Public Financing Authority (CalMuni PFA)

Contract Consent: look closer

Joins a joint powers authority that issues bonds and other financing for member cities. Membership is free and non-voting, and any actual financing would come back to the Council Verified1.

Look closer: The staff report reads like a sales pitch: "the only meaningful cost is the cost of 'not joining'." The agenda says the Mayor and City Manager sign the agreement; the report's recommendation names only the City Manager Verified1. Ask who approached the City, and who gets paid when a member city borrows through it.

H. Old business

11 Treasurer's investment report and monthly register for August 2026

Money

The monthly cash, investment and check report from Finance Director Rex Greenbaum. Hotel-tax (TOT) revenue is running well ahead of budget Verified1. Received and filed; no vote Verified2.

Look closer: The narrative says TOT is $101,987 over a $475,264 budget; the table on the next pages says $100,987 over $476,264. One page is also dated "as of August 2025" Verified1. Small errors, but this is the report the Council relies on. Read the check register: it lists every payment.

Item text: Verified1

I. New business

12 Resolution 26-37, contract 02-26 (Caltrans HSIP intersection safety project) to St. Francis Electric, $287,107

Money Contract

Awards a traffic-safety intersection contract: $207,257 base bid plus $79,850 for an optional add-on, up to $315,818 with contingency. A federal safety grant (HSIP) covers up to $288,000 of eligible costs Verified1. The City's own share, if the contingency is fully used, is up to $132,709 Calculated1.

Look closer: Only two bids. The lower bidder, Vaca Valley, was rejected as non-responsive over its subcontractor list, and the 90-day bid validity had already lapsed because of Caltrans delays Verified1. Ask for the rejection letter.

Unofficial result, from the meeting video Approved 5-0 Verified2

motion: Belmontes Leyva, second: Sellers · Video time 00:23:25–00:23:59

The rule behind this: Art. VII: conflicts of interest (Gov. Code §1090) · Williams: open government and Form 700

14 9th Street median redesign options for the E Street Complete Streets project

Money Contract

Decides whether to drop a raised median on E Street between 9th Street North and South. Staff found no collision or safety data requiring it, so it is a policy choice. Removing it adds about $10,000 to the design budget, handled as a change order on Teichert's $8,894,068 construction contract under the City Manager's existing authority Verified1.

Look closer: Staff admits the pushback from residents happened "outside of formal meetings and was not captured in meeting minutes" Verified1. Decisions shaped off the record are exactly what to watch. Ask how many change orders the $8.9 million contract has had so far, and their total.

Unofficial result, from the meeting video Approved removing the median 4-0, 1 abstention Verified2

motion: Parsons, second: Belmontes Leyva · Abstained: Mayor Dunlap · Video time 00:45:57–00:46:39

Announced as "motion carries four with one abstain." No reason for the abstention was stated in the portion we transcribed.

The rule behind this: Art. VII: conflicts of interest (Gov. Code §1090) · Williams: open government and Form 700

Item text: Verified1

15 Resolution 26-39, contract 05-26 (E Street water and sewer improvements) to Tungsten Engineering Contractors, $1,472,074

Money Contract

Awards the underground water and sewer work for E Street: $1,472,074 (14 bids; the engineer estimated $2.5 million), up to $1,619,281 with contingency Verified1.

Look closer: Two things are in the staff report but not in the agenda title: (1) $122,936.35 more for WEI's construction management (negotiated down from $290,500), for a total of $1,742,217.35; and (2) the General Fund will cover the water fund's cash shortfalls up to $800,000, to be paid back "by the end of Fiscal Year 2028" Verified1. That is a loan from the fund that pays for police, parks and everything else. Ask for the repayment schedule and what happens if it slips. A losing bidder (R&R Pacific) protested; the Council rejected the protest in a separate vote.

Unofficial result, from the meeting video Approved (after separately rejecting a bid protest) unanimous Verified2

motion: Belmontes Leyva, second: Parsons · Video time 01:03:23–01:04:23

Bid-protest rejection, voted first: motion Troughton, second Sellers, at 00:49:57–00:50:38. Ayes were heard but the count was not clear on the recording Unverified2.

The rule behind this: Art. VII: conflicts of interest (Gov. Code §1090) · Williams: open government and Form 700

Item text: Verified1

16 Resolution 26-40, business license and building fees to increase every year by CPI

Fees/rates

Business license and building fees will now rise every year with the San Francisco–Oakland–Hayward consumer price index, with no new vote each year. Staff says this fixes an omission in the 2025 fee resolution. It also adds a $2,500 deposit for repeat building reinspections, suggested by the City's building-inspection contractor, BPR Consulting Group Verified1.

Look closer: The report does not say how much more residents and businesses will pay. Automatic increases mean the Council never has to vote on them again. Ask for the dollar impact and who benefits from the reinspection deposit.

Unofficial result, from the meeting video Approved 5-0 Verified2

motion: Sellers, second: Troughton · Video time 01:06:29–01:07:10

The rule behind this: Art. XIII C: what counts as a tax · Williams: fees and rates

Item text: Verified1

17 Purchase a 2027 Ford F-450 (Water fund) and a 2027 Ford F-350 (Sewer fund); budget increases; surplus two 2010 trucks

Money

Buys two Public Works trucks from Geweke Ford: an F-450 for $94,866.92 (water fund) and an F-350 for $62,710.59 (sewer fund), and raises those budgets by $94,867 and $62,711. The old 2010 trucks go to auction. Staff says it has asked Janus Solar about a possible $85,000 donation toward the F-450 Verified1. Together: $157,577.51 Calculated1.

Look closer: A council member asked why this did not go to the finance committee first; staff said that was probably an oversight Verified2. Water and sewer funds come from your utility bills. Ask whether the Janus Solar donation came through, and whether the donor has business before the City.

Unofficial result, from the meeting video Approved 4-1 Verified2

motion: Parsons, second: Belmontes Leyva · No: Mayor Dunlap · Video time 01:14:37–01:15:17

The rule behind this: Art. XIII D §6(b): fee money pays for that service · Williams: fees and rates

Item text: Verified1

18 First Amendment to the City Manager's employment agreement (Yvonne Kimball)

Staff pay/benefits Contract Money

Eight months after her employment agreement took effect (February 19, 2026), the City Manager's contract is amended to: run five years, to February 18, 2031; raise base salary from $175,000 to $178,500; add a $500-a-month vehicle allowance; and raise severance (if fired without cause) from three months' pay to four, then five, then six months by February 2028. The City Attorney, Andreas Booher, negotiated it after the Council's direction following her August performance review Verified1.

Look closer: The agenda title just says "Approve Employment Contract Amendment for the City Manager," with no dollar figures Verified1. The stated fiscal impact is $6,500 a year, and it leaves out severance. Six months at the new salary is $89,250 Calculated1. The $500-a-month allowance is $6,000 a year, but the report counts a $3,000 increase without explaining the difference. The recommendation reads "Staff and the City Attorney recommend" adoption Verified1: city staff report to the City Manager whose pay is being set. A closed-session item was kept as a backup "in case that item didn't pass" and dropped after the vote; it does not appear on the posted agenda Verified2. The City Attorney also mentioned receiving anonymous comment letters, which were not read aloud Verified2.

Unofficial result, from the meeting video Approved 4-1 Verified2

motion: Mayor Dunlap, second: Belmontes Leyva · No: Troughton · Video time 01:40:32–01:40:48

Announced as "Motion carries 4-1." Council Member Parsons' answer was inaudible on the recording; the 4-1 count implies an aye Unverified2.
