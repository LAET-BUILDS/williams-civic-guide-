City Council, Wednesday, November 18, 2026

Regular meeting · 6:00 PM · City Hall Council Chambers, 810 E Street

Upcoming. Agenda posted; no votes yet.

Official agenda & packet (PDF)

Up to $1,003,843 for solar and battery projects at the pool and the sewer plant, without competitive bidding ($618,843 from the General Fund, $385,000 from the sewer fund) Verified1Calculated1, a $460,000 funding gap on the City's bowling alley renovation, a Recology garbage rate presentation, and the draft minutes of the October 21 meeting on consent Verified1.

Meeting: Wednesday, November 18, 2026, 6:00 PM, City Council Chambers, City Hall, 810 E Street. The agenda was posted Friday, November 13 (agenda pp. 1-3) Verified1.

How to comment. Speak in person at the podium during Public Comment (item D) on anything not on the agenda; for an agenda item, wait until the Council takes it up. Speakers are asked to give their name, whether they live in Williams, and any organization they represent, and the Mayor may set a time limit (agenda p. 1) Verified1. The agenda does not describe a way to comment from home or in writing; the livestream is a "courtesy" to watch Verified1. For a disability-related accommodation, contact City Clerk Mariana Pineda at (530) 473-2955 or (530) 235-3276, or mpineda@cityofwilliams.org, at least 48 hours before the meeting (p. 3) Verified1. More in How to speak.

Why this meeting matters. The biggest decision is item 8: about $1 million from City reserves for solar and batteries, bought without bids, with a contract that is not in the packet. The October 21 minutes (item 4) will become the official record of last month's votes, and the draft's vote tallies are inconsistent.

Next meeting. The City's printed 2026 schedule lists the next regular meeting on December 9, 2026, not the third Wednesday (p. 3) Verified1.

Public speakers are not named on this site. City officials and staff are, because they act for the public.

E. Written communications

1 Notice of intent to adopt an amended Groundwater Sustainability Plan for the Colusa Subbasin (Colusa Groundwater Authority)

The Colusa Groundwater Authority tells the City it plans to adopt an amended groundwater plan for the Colusa Subbasin, after the state Department of Water Resources recommended corrective actions. A public hearing is planned for January 2027. The letter says the Authority will consult with the City on request and takes written comments at P.O. Box 475, Colusa, CA 95932. Information only; no vote (packet pp. 7-8) Verified1.

Look closer: The City of Williams is one of the Authority's listed members. The state law quoted in the letter says the agency "shall consult with a city or county that requests consultation within 30 days of receipt of the notice"; the letter is dated Oct 9, 2026 (p. 8) Verified1. Ask whether the City asked for consultation in time, and whether it will comment before the January 2027 hearing.

Item text: Verified1

2 Letters requesting funding for the Williams Bowling Alley building, 470 7th Street

Money

The City bought the former bowling alley in 2023 with Community Development Block Grant (CDBG) program income and is renovating it as a community building. In an Oct 26, 2026 letter to Senator Megan Dahle, the City Manager asks for a one-time $460,000 grant. The letter says the project was estimated at $1,538,970, $903,970 has been spent, $635,000 is left, and the construction contract awarded in August 2026 totals $878,783. It says that if the project is abandoned the Department of Housing and Community Development would require repayment of the $903,970 already spent. Information only; no vote (packet pp. 9-11) Verified1.

Look closer: The construction contract alone is $243,783 more than the money left Calculated1. The cover report says a request was also made to former Governor Jerry Brown, but the only Brown letter in the packet is an invitation to the E Street groundbreaking, not a funding request (p. 12) Verified1. Ask what happens to the contract if no grant comes, and whether General Fund money would cover the gap.

Item text: Verified1

F. Presentation

3 Recology presentation on its annual rate increase

Fees/rates

Recology, the City's garbage hauler, presents its yearly rate increase. The packet has no staff report or rate table for this item, and the agenda lists no vote (agenda p. 2) Verified1.

Look closer: Last month the increase arrived as a letter Verified2; this month it is a presentation, and neither comes with a vote. Ask for the new rates in writing and which part of the franchise agreement allows the increase.

Lessons: Art. XIII D §6: notice, hearing, majority protest · Williams: fees and rates

Item text: Verified1

G. Consent calendar

4 Minutes of the October 21, 2026 regular meeting

Consent: look closer

Approves the written record of the October 21 meeting. The 13-page draft is in the packet; it is not official until the Council approves it (pp. 13-26) Verified1.

Look closer: Read the draft before it passes on consent. Items 5, 17 and 18 each list four ayes and one no but say the motion "passed 4-0" (pp. 15, 20-21, 23-24) Verified1. For item 15 the draft gives the contract award motion to Troughton, second Sellers, 5-0, and no tally for the separate bid-protest vote (p. 19) Verified1; our video notes heard that motion and second on the protest vote and Belmontes Leyva / Parsons on the award Verified3. The draft calls item 11 the July 2026 report; the October packet said August Verified1,2. The draft also records the City Attorney saying the item 18 staff recommendation was meant to be neutral (p. 22) Verified1. Ask the Clerk to fix the tallies before approval; once approved, these become the official votes.

Lessons: The Brown Act · Cal. Const. Art. I §3

Item text: Verified1

H. Old business

5 Treasurer's investment report and monthly register for September 2026; monthly financial status reports

Money

The monthly cash, investment and check report from Finance Director Rex Greenbaum. Cash and investments total $12,883,592; after deficits and the remaining E Street pledge, the "Adjusted General Fund Balance" is $4,351,633 (p. 29). September checks total $464,137.05 (pp. 32-34) Verified1. Received; the report asks the Council to "accept" it.

Look closer: The narrative says hotel tax (TOT) will reach $577,251, $101,987 over a $475,264 budget, the same words and numbers as the October report. This month's table says $565,576, $89,312 over $476,264 (pp. 27, 30) Verified1,2. The recommendation still says "August 2026" (p. 28), and the "Preliminary E Street Project Summary" the report says is included is not in the packet Verified1. Ask for the E Street summary and a corrected narrative.

Item text: Verified1

I. New business

6 Second reading and adoption of Ordinance 282-26, EV charging permit streamlining (new Chapter 15.33)

Final vote on the fast-track permit process for EV charging stations introduced on October 21. Staff now recommends a version updated for a 2026 state bill (canopies, "EVSE" wording), and offers "version 2," which covers only the 2015 law, as an alternative. Staff lists no immediate fiscal impact and says the Governor's Office reviewed the ordinance (pp. 35-37) Verified1. It takes effect 30 days after adoption (p. 42) Verified1.

Look closer: The recommended text is not the text introduced in October. Ask the City Attorney whether the changes are big enough to need a new first reading. The report calls the new bill both "AB 1283" and "SB 1283," and both drafts say "adopted ... on the 21st day of October, 2026," the first-reading date (pp. 35, 42, 48) Verified1. Staff also ties the vote to "the $21 million active state grants" the City uses (p. 36) Verified1.

Lessons: State and city: ordinances · Art. XI §7: city police power

Item text: Verified1

7 Rename Ella Street to Bill Vann Way (Planning Commission recommendation, Resolution 26-41)

The Williams Fire Protection Authority asked in December 2025 to rename Ella Street, where its new fire station is planned. Staff says the request was missed by "administrative oversight" until July 2026. The Planning Commission recommended it on Sept 28, 2026, 3 ayes, 1 abstention, 1 absent. Ella Street was named for a daughter of William Williams, the city's namesake. The only addressed property now is the Woodland Community College Colusa County campus; staff says no comments or objections were received. New signs cost $139.90 (pp. 53-62) Verified1.

Look closer: The cost shown is only for signs. Ask who pays for address changes for the college and the new station.

Item text: Verified1

8 Solar and battery projects at the Community Pool and the Wastewater Treatment Plant, EcoGreen Solutions, no-bid exception (Resolution 26-42)

Money Contract

A solar carport with batteries at the Community Pool ($562,675 plus a 10% contingency, from General Fund reserves) and batteries for the existing solar panels at the sewer plant ($350,000 plus 10%, from sewer fund reserves), built by EcoGreen Solutions, Inc. without competitive bidding. The City Manager would sign the agreement after City Attorney review. Staff expects 50% to 60% back as a federal tax-credit payment if the systems run by Dec 31, 2027, but plans on 45%, and projects payback in 11 years (pool) and 9 years (sewer plant), but says the incentives "are not guaranteed" (pp. 63-70) Verified1.

Look closer: The amounts disagree: the agenda and staff report say $618,843 for the pool; Resolution 26-42 says $619,163 and a $1,004,163 total (pp. 2, 64, 66-67) Verified1; $562,675 plus the stated $56,268 contingency is $618,943 Calculated1. The staff report says the city code normally requires bids over $200,000 Verified1. Staff's own report says the no-bid findings must rest on "documentation addressing the availability of other qualified contractors," and that time pressure and past experience "alone are not sufficient" (p. 64) Verified1, but the packet has no such documentation and no EcoGreen agreement, only the resolution and two savings tables (p. 65) Verified1. The report also mentions "four municipal facilities" for a two-site project and cites fund balances from June 30, 2025 Verified1. Ask to see the agreement, the market documentation and the other bids or quotes before the vote.

Lessons: Art. VII: conflicts of interest (Gov. Code §1090) · Williams: open government and Form 700
